# Decision Architecture
## How to put "The Last Meter" in front of the office that can fund it

**Date:** 14 November 2026
**Status:** research complete, one open question unresolved (§8)
**Purpose:** convert a competition entry into the technical annex of a decision that has a date, an approver, and a budget line

---

## 0. The one-paragraph version

Manila already has the law, the money, and the assigned duty. Ordinance No. 8491 requires every household to desludge every three to five years and makes free residential desludging every five years an obligation of the water concessionaire; the concession agreement sets a five-to-seven-year cycle; and every water bill already carries an environmental charge — raised from 20% to 25% in January 2025 — that pays for it. Yet Maynilad emptied **98,216 tanks in 2024** against roughly **1.53 million billed accounts**, which is about **6.4% a year, a cycle near fifteen years.** The mandate is not missing. The delivery is. That gap, at the last metre of a doorstep, is the subject of this entry — and the decision that closes it is made in **April to June 2027**, when Manila approves its FY2028 Annual Investment Plan.

---

## 1. The decision this entry is written for

A ₱10 million, twelve-month, locally funded pilot does not touch the National Expenditure Program, the General Appropriations Act, or NEDA's Investment Coordination Committee. **The ICC threshold is ₱5 billion** for locally funded projects, raised from ₱2.5 billion in October 2025 — four hundred times this pilot's budget.

It has exactly one binding door.

| Step | Date | Authority | What it decides |
|---|---|---|---|
| Local Finance Committee revenue projections | Jan–Mar 2027 | RA 7160 §316 | The ceiling |
| Department proposals | Jan–Mar 2027 | §317 | What departments ask for |
| **LDC prioritises PPAs → AIP drafted** | **Apr–May 2027** | §109(a)(3); JMC 1 s.2016 §10.3.3 | **Whether the pilot exists** |
| **AIP approved** | **on or before 7 June 2027** | §305(i) | The binding act |
| Executive budget submitted | 16 Oct 2027 | §318 | The amount |
| Appropriation ordinance | by 31 Dec 2027 | §319 | Spending authority |
| Effective | 1 Jan 2028 | §320 | Money exists |

**Manila is a highly urbanised city, so there is no DBM review of its budget.** §327 covers component cities and municipalities only. Once the Sangguniang Panlungsod enacts the ordinance and the Mayor signs it, it stands, subject only to COA post-audit. This is unusual and useful: a single city can commit this money without national clearance.

**Two consequences for the competition schedule.**

First, the Phase II presentation — roughly mid-2027 — lands **six weeks before the April drafting window.** The paper is not describing a hypothetical future. It is the document that would be read while the AIP is being written.

Second, the last realistic month to insert a new line item is **April 2027**. Later than that, you are asking for a mention in the budget message, not a line. The §321 supplemental-budget fallback requires *new or certified available* funds and should not be planned around.

> **The strategic claim:** this entry is not a proposal for a programme. It is the technical annex to an AIP line item, written nine months early.

## 2. Why this is an enforcement gap, not a service gap

Four instruments already exist. This section is the spine of the revised rationale.

| Instrument | What it already requires | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Ordinance No. 8491 (2016)** — Septage Management System of Manila | Desludge every 3–5 years or at one-third sludge volume; tanks kept accessible; Environmental Sanitation Certificate required for building and business permits; **free residential desludging every five years by the water concessionaires**, excess charged at 10% of basic consumption | Enacted |
| **Ordinance No. 8793 (2021)** — Sanitation and Disinfection Code | Desludging certificate from a DENR-accredited transporter as proof of treatment | Enacted |
| **Revised Concession Agreement** | Domestic septic tanks emptied "at regular intervals of five (5) to seven (7) years" | In force |
| **Environmental charge on the water bill** | 20% of the bill, raised to **25% from January 2025** | Collected |

The City Environment and Natural Resources Office (CENRO) maintains a desludging database with the City Planning and Development Office, accredits haulers, and issues sanitation clearances. The Sanitary Engineer heads the Sanitation Division under the Manila Health Department. The City Building Official certifies tank access at permit stage.

So the chain is: **law written, money collected, obligation assigned, registry kept, permit condition imposed.** The failure is downstream of all of it.

### The arithmetic that carries the argument

| Quantity | Figure | Source status |
|---|---:|---|
| Tanks desludged, 2024 | 98,216 | Published (Maynilad 2024 Integrated Report) |
| Billed accounts, west zone | ~1.53 million | Published |
| Implied annual rate | **6.4%** | **Derived — show the division** |
| Implied cycle | **~15.6 years** | **Derived** |
| Legal cycle | 5–7 years | Concession Agreement |
| Accounts *offered* desludging, 2022–2026 | 801,221 | Published (2024 SEC 17-A) |

**Say the derivation out loud in the paper.** 98,216 ÷ 1,530,000 = 6.4% per year; 1 ÷ 0.064 = 15.6 years. A derived number presented as a published one is the fastest way to lose the references mark.

**The trap this exposes.** Maynilad reports *coverage* as accounts **offered** desludging within a rate-rebasing cycle — 801,221 for 2022–2026. *Offered* is not *emptied*. There is no published reconciliation between the two, no septage-specific performance target in MWSS Regulatory Office public monitoring, and no septage-specific penalty. The RO does fine the concessionaire — ₱54.28 million in bill rebates in April 2026 — but for supply interruptions, not sanitation.

The only septage target on record is the old one: **regular desludging of 80% of customers by 2021** (Eawag case study). Against a 15.6-year implied cycle, that target is not close.

### The indicator that does not exist

No Philippine agency publishes **"households desludged"** as a standing indicator. Not the DOH Field Health Services Information System, which reports sanitation *facility access* by city. Not the PSA. Not the DILG. Not the MWSS RO.

That absence is the contribution. The pilot does not merely measure itself — it **builds the indicator the regulator should already have**, and gets the City Health Office to adopt it. That is what Deliverable 4 asks for and almost no entry will attempt.

## 3. The four doors in

Standing is the hard part. A student team has no seat on any local special body. Here is what actually exists.

| Door | Legal basis | What it decides | Can you enter it? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LDC technical secretariat** | §113 — the LDC may engage an NGO or **an educational or research institution** as its secretariat | Shapes what reaches the AIP | **Yes.** The cleanest hook. UP as secretariat, engaged by resolution |
| **Local Development Council** | §107(b) — NGO/PO representation not less than one-fourth | Appraises and prioritises PPAs → the AIP | Not as students. Requires sanggunian CSO accreditation: SEC/CDA registration, ≥1 year operating, selection by the accredited-CSO assembly (DILG MC 2025-060) |
| **Local Health Board** | §102(b)(1) — proposes annual budgetary allocations for health services to the sanggunian | A formal proposal, citable on the floor | Observers only; one accredited private-sector/NGO health seat |
| **Barangay assembly** | §397, §398 — residents 15+, six months' residency; meets twice yearly, any Saturday or Sunday of **March and October** | Recommends measures; hears the semestral report | **You can attend and, at the chair's discretion, speak.** The one room you can physically enter |

**Two doors that do not exist.** There is **no mandated public hearing on the AIP or the budget** — hearings are required for land reclassification and zoning, not appropriations. Sanggunian sessions are open to the public under §52(c) but committee hearings are by invitation, with no right to be heard. And participatory budgeting is gone: Bottom-Up Budgeting was dropped in 2016, and its successor facility is restricted to municipalities for water supply, which excludes Manila.

**So the route is: barangay resolution → City Health Office endorsement → LDC secretariat engagement → AIP line item.** Not a petition. A technical annex, handed to the office that drafts the document.

### The money already sitting there

§287 requires LGUs to set aside **20% of their national tax allotment as a Development Fund**, and sanitation is a qualifying use. Unspent development fund is a standing COA finding. A city looking for a compliant, defensible, environmental-tagged use of development fund is a city that will read a costed proposal.

That is the funding argument, and it is stronger than "please fund our pilot."

## 4. The signature chain

Twelve offices. In order. Each needs something specific.

| # | Office | What it gives | What it must see | Realistic lead time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Barangay council** | Resolution, captain's endorsement | Concept note, household roster, schedule, access and road plan | 2–4 weeks |
| 2 | **Manila Health Department** (Sanitary Engineer, Sanitation Division) | Sanitary inspection; hauler and environmental sanitation clearance; health IEC | Desludging protocol, accredited hauler, disposal route, incidental-exposure plan | 3–6 weeks |
| 3 | **Office of the Building Official** | Tank accessibility certification under Ord. 8491 | Access certification; no structure over the tank | 2–3 weeks |
| 4 | **City Administrator / Office of the Mayor** | MOA, city counterpart, authority to proceed | Full package plus legal review | 4–8 weeks |
| 5 | **Sangguniang Panlungsod** | Resolution concurring in the MOA, or appropriating | Committee referral; health and environment committees | 4–10 weeks |
| 6 | **Maynilad** | Service MOA, truck schedule, treatment-facility acceptance | Household count, map, volume estimate | 4–8 weeks — **and it can simply decline a schedule outside its plan** |
| 7 | **MWSS Regulatory Office** | Confirmation the activity counts toward the service obligation | Consistency with the 2023–2027 business plan | 4–6 weeks |
| 8 | **DENR-EMB NCR** | Transporter registration, discharge permit, manifest | Receiving-facility permit, hauling route | 4–8 weeks |
| 9 | **MMDA** | Road and estero works clearance, traffic management | Traffic plan, dredging and clearing clearance | 2–4 weeks |
| 10 | **PRCMO / DENR-NCR** | Only if the reach touches the easement or bank | LIAC-Manila coordination | Case by case |
| 11 | **DPWH Manila district** | Only for excavation on national roads (Quirino, Jesus, Nagtahan) | Works permit | Case by case |
| 12 | **NHA / PCUP / DHSUD** | Only if informal structures are affected | Relocation plan | Case by case |

**Read the chain for what it tells you.** The gatekeepers are the **barangay** and the **City Health Office**. Maynilad is a veto on scheduling, not an approver of legality. Everything after item 6 is a consequence of the first five.

**Put this table in the paper.** It is the single most concrete demonstration of "practical execution" available, and it converts the rubric's Practicality descriptor — *"readily feasible and can be replicated with relative ease"* — from an assertion into an artifact.

## 5. The compliance audit: the primary research instrument

This replaces "we will conduct a survey." It is the one piece of primary data no other entry will have, and it is legally framed rather than sociologically framed.

### What to measure, in the reach

| Item | Instrument | Who holds it | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tank location and access tier (F1/F2/F3) | Walked census; tape; photograph with consent | You | The only access measurement at this scale in the country |
| Year of last desludging | Household interview; hauler receipt if kept | Household | The compliance numerator |
| Whether a receipt or manifest exists | Request to see it | Household | Distinguishes "done" from "recorded" |
| Scheduled or on-demand service | Maynilad schedule; barangay log | Maynilad, barangay | Tests whether the programme ever reached this block |
| Sewer connection status | Physical inspection; CPDO service area map | CPDO | See §8 — this can decide the thesis |
| Structures over tanks | Inspection; Building Official records | OBO | The §9 permit-condition failure |

### The finding this produces

Not "access is a barrier." Rather:

> Of *n* households in the reach, *a* had been desludged within five years, against a statutory duty on all of them. Of the *b* that had not, *c* had a tank requiring floor works and *d* had no record of ever being scheduled. The city's registry shows *e*. The gap between *e* and *a* is the enforcement gap.

That is a finding with a denominator, a legal standard, and an implementer. It is auditable. It is what the rubric's Implications section is asking for and what a judge can check.

### Do not

Do not present it as an audit *of* Maynilad. The framing is a **compliance baseline for the city's own ordinance**, produced with the concessionaire's cooperation. An adversarial audit loses door 6 permanently.

## 6. The scaling rule

Every entry will write "then we expand to other cities." That is the 1/10 answer: *"The overall proposal is generic and widely used as a strategy."*

The 10/10 answer is a **decision rule a different LGU could execute without you.** Four conditions, three regimes:

> **A city is ready for replication when it has (a) a desludging mandate on the books, (b) a concessionaire under a five-to-seven-year obligation, (c) a published tank registry, and (d) an unspent development fund.**
>
> - **All four hold → the intervention is enforcement.** The pilot's job is access remediation and the two-arm protocol; no new money for service is needed.
> - **(a) and (b) hold, (d) fails → it is procurement.** The budget argument changes from development fund to a tariff or subsidy case.
> - **(c) fails → the first year is registry-building, not desludging.** Expect a pilot that measures its own baseline and desludges nothing until month six.

That paragraph turns the pilot into a **readiness test**, and a readiness test is something a national agency can adopt as an instrument.

### Applying it to the three named rivers

The kit names Pasig, Tullahan, and Meycauayan, and lists **Estero de Pandacan** as a key Pasig tributary. Paco and Pandacan sit in the same district cluster and share outfall logic — say so explicitly, because the kit has already nominated the segment you are working next to.

| River system | Regime | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Pasig** — Estero de Paco → Pandacan → Binondo | Enforcement | Manila's ordinance and Maynilad's obligation both hold; the constraint is access |
| **Tullahan** — Valenzuela, Caloocan, Quezon City | Procurement or enforcement, depending on the LGU | Same concessionaire, different city ordinances; check each |
| **Meycauayan** — Bulacan | **Not yet.** Industrial co-contamination | A household programme cannot answer a metals problem. Screen first |

That last row is an exclusion, and stating an exclusion is what makes the other two credible.

## 8. The open question that can end the thesis

**Paco is listed among Manila's sewered districts**, alongside Tondo, Sampaloc, and Sta. Cruz. Maynilad's citywide sewerage coverage is 35%.

This cuts both ways and must be resolved before the paper is finalised.

**If most reach households are unconnected** — likely, given 35% coverage — they fall between two programmes: the septage programme does not schedule them because the district is nominally sewered, and the sewerage programme has not reached them. **They are administratively invisible and legally owed.** That is "The Last Meter" in its purest form, and it is a better thesis than generic access.

**If they are connected**, the septage framing is wrong for this site, and the reach should move to an unsewered barangay in the same cluster.

### The test, and it costs one day

1. Request the **sewerage service area map** from CPDO, and the connection register for the reach.
2. Walk the reach. Count sewer manholes, and count households plausibly connected to each.
3. Inspect ten tanks for a sewer lateral at the outlet.
4. Ask the barangay whether the block has ever appeared on a Maynilad desludging schedule.

**Decision rule:** if fewer than roughly a third of reach households show a sewer lateral, the septage thesis holds and the "invisible households" framing becomes the paper's opening. If more, move the reach.

Log this as **G1b** in the gate checklist. It sits alongside G1, and it is decisive.

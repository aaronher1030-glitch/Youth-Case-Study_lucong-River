# AIP Pilot Line Item
## The ₱10 million entry, written in the format the City Budget Office actually uses

**Date:** 14 November 2026
**Companion to:** [`2026-11-14-decision-architecture.md`](2026-11-14-decision-architecture.md)
**Purpose:** a costed, tagged, resolution-backed PPA that can be lifted into Manila's FY2028 Annual Investment Plan without rewriting

---

## 0. Why this document exists

The rubric's Practicality descriptor is *"readily feasible and can be replicated with relative ease; all within the confines of the provided scope."* The Economics descriptor is *"a detailed economic analysis… confined within the allotted budget."*

Both are satisfied by **format compliance**, not by prose. A city budget officer does not read a narrative; they read a row with eight columns and a number in thousands. This document is that row, plus the arithmetic behind it.

If the entry is submitted with a PDF of a budget table that no LGU could ingest, it has failed the feasibility test regardless of how good the idea is.

**PS is zero, and that is a decision, not an omission.** All personnel are engaged by contract of service, which is charged to MOOE. A plantilla version would move ₱2,167,000 (lines 3.1 and 12.1) from MOOE to PS, changing the class split but not the total or the 70/30 test. **State this explicitly** — a reviewer who sees PS = 0 without explanation will assume an error.

### The 70/30 test

The kit requires at least **70% to one main focus stream**, with up to 30% for a supporting secondary activity.

- Stream 1 = ₱7,037,000 = **70.37%** ✓
- Stream 2 plus contingency = ₱2,963,000 = **29.63%** ✓

Contingency is charged wholly to the secondary envelope, which is the stricter convention. Even so, the primary clears the floor. **Say that in one sentence in §8 of the paper** — compliance stated once, plainly, reads as competence; compliance argued at length reads as anxiety.

## 3. The question a budget reviewer will ask first

> *"If Maynilad is already obligated to desludge residential tanks free of charge every five years, why is the city buying a vacuum unit?"*

This must be answered in the description, not in a footnote. The answer:

**The city is not buying a service. It is buying reach.**

Maynilad hauls in 10 m³ tankers down streets a tanker can enter. The access survey found 71% of paths fit only a person or a motorbike. A 1–2 m³ trailer unit is not a smaller version of the service — it is the only vehicle that reaches the 29% of tanks the tanker cannot, and it exists to close a gap the concession obligation cannot close on its own geometry.

Two supporting points:

- **The environmental charge already pays for the service.** 25% of every water bill since January 2025. The pilot is not duplicating a funded activity; it is funding the last metre that the funded activity cannot reach.
- **If Maynilad's schedule reaches the block, use it.** The pilot's unit is a fallback for the sub-set the tanker cannot serve. Where the concessionaire will haul, the ₱470,000 charter and ₱370,000 gate fee fall away, and the Stream 1 total drops to ₱6,197,000. Report that as a **sensitivity, not a saving** — it is contingent on a schedule the city does not control.

That last line matters. It shows you have modelled the interaction with the concessionaire instead of assuming it away.

| **Barangay counterpart** | Labour, venue, assembly logistics | Non-cash | Low |

**The NSSMP row is the important one.** It is tempting to cite a 50% national subsidy. It funds sewerage and septage *facilities* — treatment plants, not trucks and not household service — and by 2025 only two LGUs nationwide held subsidy approval. Claiming it would be the single easiest error for a reviewer to catch.

## 5. Draft Sanggunian resolution

> **A RESOLUTION AUTHORIZING THE CITY MAYOR TO ENTER INTO A MEMORANDUM OF AGREEMENT FOR THE IMPLEMENTATION OF A HOUSEHOLD SEPTAGE ACCESS AND COMPLIANCE PILOT ALONG ESTERO DE PACO, AND FOR OTHER PURPOSES**
>
> **WHEREAS**, Ordinance No. 8491 establishes a Septage Management System in the City of Manila and requires the desludging of septic tanks at intervals of three to five years;
>
> **WHEREAS**, Section 11 of the said Ordinance provides for free residential desludging every five years by the water concessionaires, the cost of which is recovered through the environmental charge;
>
> **WHEREAS**, notwithstanding the said mandate, the rate of desludging in the City remains below the statutory interval, and no city-level indicator of household desludging currently exists;
>
> **WHEREAS**, a one-kilometre pilot along Estero de Paco will establish an access-tier register, test two notification protocols, and produce a baseline and twelve-month target for a city desludging indicator, within a budget of Ten Million Pesos (₱10,000,000) chargeable to the 20% Development Fund;
>
> **NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED**, as it is hereby resolved, to authorize the City Mayor to enter into a Memorandum of Agreement with the implementing partners, subject to applicable procurement, audit, and accounting rules.
>
> *Note: verify the section number in the second Whereas against the enrolled copy of Ordinance No. 8491. The official PDF is a scanned image; secondary sources give §11 but this has not been confirmed on the page.*

**Why a resolution and not an ordinance.** An ordinance creates a duty; a resolution authorises an act. The duty already exists — Ord. 8491 — so a new ordinance would be redundant and signals the team did not read the city's own code. A resolution is also faster and does not require the same committee track.

## 7. Indicator definition sheet

The deliverable that does not exist anywhere in Philippine government reporting. Written to FHSIS conventions so the City Health Office can adopt it without translation.

| Field | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Indicator name** | Households with septic tank desludged within the last five years |
| **Numerator** | Households in the catchment whose tank was emptied, evidenced by a dated hauler manifest or facility receipt, within 60 months of the reference date |
| **Denominator** | All households in the catchment with an on-site septic tank, excluding those connected to a sewer |
| **Unit** | Percentage of households |
| **Reference period** | Rolling 60 months |
| **Frequency** | Annual |
| **Disaggregation** | By barangay; by access tier (F1/F2/F3); by tenure (owner/renter) |
| **Data source** | City desludging database (CENRO–CPDO); hauler manifests; household verification sample |
| **Verification** | 10% random household re-check against manifest; discrepancy rate reported |
| **Why five years** | The statutory interval in Ordinance No. 8491 and the concession obligation |
| **Why not "sanitation coverage"** | Coverage measures toilet or facility type. It says nothing about whether the tank has ever been emptied, which is the variable that governs discharge |

**The last row is the argument.** Every existing Philippine sanitation indicator measures the *fixture*. None measures the *emptying*. A city can report near-universal sanitation coverage while its tanks overflow. That sentence alone justifies the pilot.

### Baseline and targets

| Measure | Baseline | 12-month target |
|---|---|---|
| Households desludged within 5 years | Measured in Month 2 — **not assumed** | ≥80% in Arm 2; report both arms |
| Arm 2 minus Arm 1 | Not measured anywhere | Report with 95% interval |
| Manifest–receipt match | Not measured | 100% |
| Access tier recorded | 0% | 100% of reach |
| Cost per household served | ₱25,000 year one (capital-inclusive) | Report recurring cost separately |

**Do not carry a baseline you have not measured.** The first row is blank on purpose, and the paper says so. An invented baseline is worse than an absent one, because it invites a question the team cannot answer.

## 8. The handover package

What the team physically gives the city in Month 12. This is the Community Involvement and sustainability answer, and it should be listed, not described.

1. **Access-tier register** — every tank in the reach, geolocated, tiered, with the remediation needed. Format: the city's own desludging database schema, not a custom one.
2. **Indicator definition and one year of data** — §7 above, populated.
3. **Protocol pack** — register, scheduling, chain of custody, alarm response, safety, evaluation. Six documents, already written.
4. **Cost model** — the 16-line budget with actuals against estimates, so the next barangay can be costed in an afternoon.
5. **Named operator and budget holder** — or the equipment is removed and the register transferred. State the closure rule in the paper; it is the difference between a pilot and a grant.
6. **A signed MOA that survives the team.** The team leaves in June. The instrument must not.

## 9. What not to put in the submission

| Temptation | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| A Gantt chart of the team's own work | The kit asks for a 12-month *implementation* plan, not a project-management chart. Keep that internal |
| A line for "research and documentation" | Reads as paying yourself. Fold into analysis and closeout |
| A 5-year budget | The seed is a 12-month proof of concept. A multi-year ask fails Practicality |
| Any NSSMP subsidy | Funds facilities, not operations |
| A cost per household that omits capital | ₱5,070/hh is a recurring figure; ₱25,000/hh includes the unit. Report both, labelled |
| A new ordinance | Ord. 8491 already exists |

**That is the whole argument of this strategy:** the marginal return on format is far higher than the marginal return on more literature.

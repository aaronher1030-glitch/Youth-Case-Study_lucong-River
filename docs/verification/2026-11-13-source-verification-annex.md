# Source Verification Annex
## The Last Meter — claim-by-claim audit
### Version 1.0 · 13 November 2026

> **Purpose.** Nine claims in draft v0.1 failed or were misstated on verification. This annex records each failure, the corrected wording, and the exact citation. A human author should be able to defend every sentence in the paper from this document alone.
>
> **Rule for use:** if a claim is marked **CORRECTED** or **WITHDRAWN**, the v0.1 wording may not appear in the submission.

## 1. Corrections — claims that were wrong

### C1. Fecal coliform Class C limit — **wrong value**

| | |
|---|---|
| v0.1 said | "fecal coliform of 400 MPN/100 mL or less" for Class C |
| Correct | **200 MPN/100 mL** for Class C ambient water quality |
| Why the error | 400 is the Class C **General Effluent Standard** (discharge limit), not the ambient guideline |
| Citation | DENR (2016), DAO 2016-08, **Table 3**, p. 6; DAO 2021-19 Table 4 confirms 200 |
| Action | Replace in §1. If comparing to effluent, say "General Effluent Standard of 400" explicitly |

**This changes the arithmetic.** Measured Manila City values of 2.4×10⁵–1.1×10⁶ MPN/100 mL are **1,200 to 5,500 times** the Class C guideline, not 600–2,750 times.

### C2. PEMSEA 83.44% — **wrong geographic scope**

| | |
|---|---|
| v0.1 said | 83.4% of BOD in "the Pasig basin" is domestic |
| Correct | 83.44% of BOD across the **entire Laguna de Bay–Pasig River–Manila Bay watershed**, 58 sub-basins, 232,764 MT/yr, 2008 baseline |
| Why it matters | It is not a Pasig-only apportionment |
| Citation | Hernandez & Gonzales (2013), p. 21, Fig. 6 |
| Action | Rewrite as "the whole Laguna de Bay–Pasig–Manila Bay watershed" |

**It is also explicitly a model:** "estimated using the unit load approach… a **first pass estimate**… computed onsite **without considering the natural purification** in river systems." Quote that caveat, or a judge will find it.

### C3. The 13% desludging rate — **misdescribed scope, and probably stale**

| | |
|---|---|
| v0.1 said | "an 800-household survey of low-income Metro Manila" |
| Correct source | **IFC (2018), *Expanding Access to Improved Sanitation for the Poor: Insights from the Philippines*** — not "Improving Sanitation Services in the Philippines" |
| Actual scope | 800 households across **Metro Manila, Rizal, Laguna and Pampanga**; 12 municipalities; **only 18% below the poverty line**; June–July 2016 |
| Exact wording | "only 13 percent of septic tanks have ever been desludged… more than 50 percent of systems having operated for longer than five years and a mean age of nine years" (p. 6) |
| Denominator | Tanks among the ~400 private-toilet households, not all 800 |
| Action | Cite as a **four-province 2016 comparator**, never as "Metro Manila" or "low-income" |

**And it likely no longer holds.** Concessionaire data show 182,559 tanks desludged in 2017 rising to **225,600 in 2024**. Against roughly 2.2 million tanks, that implies a mean interval near ten years — which makes "only 13% ever emptied" in 2026 implausible. Do not build the argument on it.

### C4. "Access is the binding constraint" — **not supported**

Three findings break it:

- **Standard vacuum tankers carry 100 metres of hose** (two 20 m of 4-inch, four 20 m of 3-inch). The statistic "18% of toilets more than 20 metres from a truck" therefore describes households that are, in fact, **within normal reach**.
- **Baliwag Water District**, a flat town with wide roads and no access constraint, had only **20.09% desludged**, with 77.21% "still to be desludged."
- **MWSS acceptance** of free desludging was **56.96% (2024)** and **55.16% (2025)**, missing a 60% target. Roughly **40% of customers — 6.88 million of 17.21 million — waive the service.**

Meanwhile the regulator's own framing names a different cause: **"7M Metro Manila residents unaware of free septic tank desludging."** MWSS funds an "Awareness Survey for Desludging Services" in its 2025 scorecard. Palanca-Tan (2017) found **9.3% of households still paid private desludgers ₱1,300–7,000** rather than wait for the free service.

**Conclusion: the access hypothesis is credible but untested. Treat it as a hypothesis, not a finding.**

### C5. ADB Barangay 672 — **headline result is selective**

| | |
|---|---|
| v0.1 said | "reached 87% BOD removal across three months of monitoring" |
| Missing | Coliform fell only **10⁷ → 10⁵ MPN/100 mL — about 20% removal, roughly 250× the Class C limit** |
| Also | Barangay 672 had **58 households and only 7 substandard tanks**; 51 of 58 discharged directly. Septage collection is irrelevant for 88% of that barangay |
| Action | Report both BOD and coliform. Exclude Barangay 672 from the cohort or justify its inclusion |

### C6. CCME water quality index — **source contradicts itself**

The paper states "only marginal, decimal-level changes" for 2025–2030, then says the same projection shows "an almost 10-point improvement across the six stations." **Quote both, or the claim is cherry-picked.**

### C7. Portable desludging unit — **not available to this project**

| | |
|---|---|
| Reality | Manila Water's **patented prototype**, IPOPHL award March 2025; capacity 1 m³; **two units deployed**; "pricing… not finalized… still in its prototype stage" |
| Worse | **Paco is in Maynilad's West Zone**, not Manila Water's East Zone |
| Action | Do not budget it as purchasable. Either budget a **purchased 1–2 m³ vacuum unit** or have the concessionaire provide equipment |

### C8. Budget line — **tanker cannot be bought**

Maynilad paid **₱179 million for 19 × 10 m³ vacuum trucks** (2020), or **≈₱9.4 million each**. A purchased tanker would consume the entire allocation. **Charter hauling instead.**

### C9. Emptying interval vs load — **river claims must be withdrawn**

A 1.58 m³ tank emptied once a decade releases about **0.43 litres per day**. Four occupants discharge roughly **40 g BOD/day as effluent** regardless. Full compliance therefore abates a small fraction of one tank's BOD flux — and the corridor outside the cohort keeps discharging.

**Action: withdraw every claim that the pilot will change river load. The primary endpoint is tanks desludged and manifests reconciled.**

## 3. Budget rebuild

The v0.1 budget assumed a portable unit that does not exist for sale and a tanker that costs ₱9.4 million.

| Line | Basis | Amount ₱ |
|---|---|---:|
| 1–2 m³ trailer vacuum unit, purchased | FOB US$23,000–40,000 comparables | 2,000,000 |
| Access remediation, 400 × 3,000 | Component costing; **no published unit rate exists** | 1,200,000 |
| Crew, 8 × 234 days × 755 × 1.25 | NCR-28 wage | 1,767,000 |
| Hauling charter, 100 loads × 4,700 | Maynilad published rate | 470,000 |
| Septage gate fee, 632 m³ × 580 | Ormoc City ordinance benchmark | 370,000 |
| Hoses, PPE, spill kits | Allowance | 400,000 |
| Register and manifest system | Allowance | 350,000 |
| Barangay coordination | Allowance | 300,000 |
| Chain-of-custody audits | Allowance | 180,000 |
| **Direct (70.4%)** | | **7,037,000** |
| Float alarms, 400 × 1,500 | Retail listings | 600,000 |
| Telemetry | Allowance | 120,000 |
| Observation and evaluation staff | Allowance | 400,000 |
| Laboratory, 5 × 12 × 3,000 | **unverified package price** | 180,000 |
| Survey, permits, legal | Allowance | 300,000 |
| Analysis and closeout | Allowance | 200,000 |
| Contingency | | 1,163,000 |
| **Total** | | **10,000,000** |

**Four lines have no published Philippine benchmark** and must be labelled assumptions in the paper: access remediation, the portable unit, the register system, and the gate fee.

**Economics must be stated honestly.** At 400 households, recurring service cost is roughly ₱7,000 per household per year, against a US$10–30 benchmark and an environmental-charge yield of roughly ₱500–1,000. **The pilot does not close that gap. It measures it, and the gap is the finding.**

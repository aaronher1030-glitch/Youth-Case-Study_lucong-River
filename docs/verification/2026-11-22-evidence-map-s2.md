## Paragraph blueprint check

| ¶ | Role as written | Role the chain wants | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Context: river, reach, barangays | Context | Good |
| 2 | Severity: monitoring data | Severity | Good |
| 3 | Pressure conditions on the banks | Method landscape | **List-shaped**: six bank uses in a row |
| 4 | Attribution and diagnosis | Bottleneck | Good |
| 5 | Test plan | Gap | Good |
| 6 | The rule that failed | Contribution boundary | Good, but arrives last |

Two findings follow from the check.

1. **¶3 fails the anti-enumeration gate.** Housing, shops, paddies, septic tanks, trucks, drains, piggeries — the paragraph can be converted into a table without losing meaning. One connective clause would fix it: the point is that all six deposit into a channel with no treatment, not that six things exist.
2. **The bottleneck step is missing from the middle of §2.** The chain wants "existing technical routes and where they fail" before the gap. In this paper that material sits in §4 (digesters, Naga). That is the right place for an eight-part case study, but a marker reading §2 alone sees a diagnosis and then a rationale with no account of what has already been tried.

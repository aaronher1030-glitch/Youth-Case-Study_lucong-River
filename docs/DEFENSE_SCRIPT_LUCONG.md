# Research Defense Script: Eighty Times the Limit
### Speaking script and Q&A guide — Lucong River, Concepcion, Tarlac

**How to use this document.** Part 1 is a **4-to-5-minute spoken script** divided into six short beats so one or multiple team members can present smoothly without reading dense paragraphs. Part 2 gives **one-breath answers** to the hardest questions judges are likely to ask, grounded strictly in the verified numbers from the paper and data brief.

## Part 1. Spoken Presentation Script (~650 words, 4–5 minutes)

**1. Opening and the problem (45 seconds)**

Good morning, panel. Our case study asks a practical question about the **Lucong River** in Concepcion, Tarlac—listed as *Lucung* in the municipal record.

Between Santiago Bridge and Alfonso Bridge lies a **1.52-kilometre urban reach** bordered by Barangays Santiago and Alfonso, home to **15,220 residents** across **3,148 households**. In the **October 2024 to 2025 Environmental Management Bureau (EMB) monitoring round**, fecal coliform at Santiago Bridge reached **16,000 MPN per 100 mL**—**80 times** the Class C limit of 200.

Yet every other measured parameter—BOD at 5 mg/L, dissolved oxygen at 6.7 mg/L, pH at 7.83, and temperature at 30.4 °C—stayed inside DAO 2016-08 standards. That chemistry tells us the river is not choked by high-oxygen-demand industrial effluent. The signal is **fecal**.

**2. Diagnosis and why rules alone failed (45 seconds)**

Nothing along this bank is plumbed to a sewage treatment plant. Households use septic tanks, solid waste still gathers below the bridge, and commercial livestock sheds discharge into a channel with no collection system. EMB's own report points to domestic wastewater, agricultural runoff, and livestock farms discharging into the Lucong.

Passing another rule will not fix this by itself. In **2017**, Concepcion's Sangguniang Bayan ordered a **10,000-head piggery** 500 metres from the town centre closed, only for the provincial board to void the order because the underlying zoning ordinance had never been approved. Even in cities with strict rules—like **Naga City's 2017 ordinance**, which mandates biogas digesters on commercial farms—no municipal service exists to actually haul manure off-site.

**3. Our solution: a service, not just a device (60 seconds)**

That is why **our product is a shed-level waste service**, funded 100 per cent under **Stream 2** for commercial and industrial pollution.

We rejected a piped sewage treatment plant because building a sewer network across unsewered barangays is impossible inside **₱10 million**. Instead, we collect concentrated waste right at the piggery shed before it dilutes into the river:

1. **At the shed:** Each participating shed gets a sealed concrete sump and a 200-litre drum so raw manure and washwater never reach the bank.
2. **Weekly haulage:** A **2,000-litre vacuum tanker** collects slurry weekly on a fixed route, weighed and logged on a **countersigned manifest**.
3. **Central treatment:** At one central yard, **three 25-cubic-metre covered anaerobic digesters** process an estimated **450 kg of manure and 5 cubic metres of washwater per day** for 10 to 15 days. This captures roughly **30 cubic metres of biogas a day**—about **18 kg of LPG equivalent**—to heat washwater and shed scalding tanks.
4. **Nutrient return:** Dewatered digestate is mixed 1-to-3 with local rice husk and composted under a roof for 45 days, then returned free in Year 1 to the rice farms behind the levees.

**4. Where the innovation sits (30 seconds)**

The innovation is **not** the digester—covered lagoons have operated on Philippine farms for decades. The innovation is **attaching haulage to an existing permit gate**. Concepcion's 2024 Citizen's Charter already requires barangay clearance and health certificates for a piggery's Mayor's permit. Under our design, a shed keeps its permit only if every load is collected and signed off on a **manifest** by a barangay monitor—turning enforcement and service into one verifiable transaction.

**5. Plan, budget, and realistic targets (60 seconds)**

The project runs **24 months** inside a **₱10,000,000 budget**—₱9.2 million in works and operations plus an ₱800,000 contingency, with **zero compost revenue** assumed so we never rely on an untested market.

Months 1 to 3 verify our assumptions first: we census the sheds with MENRO, sample above and below piggery clusters, GPS every drain, and secure site consent. At our planning estimate of **15 sheds** (about 180 head), public cost is roughly **₱25,600 per shed-month** and diverts **164 tonnes of manure a year**; if scaled to 60 sheds, fixed costs spread down to **₱6,400 per shed-month**.

We commit to publishing four quarterly indicators: **95 per cent manifest coverage**, **164 tonnes/year diverted**, actual **cost per shed-month**, and **fecal coliform**—targeting **2,000 MPN/100 mL by Month 24**, an **88 per cent cut**, and **200 by Month 36** if the piggery share holds.

**6. Safety, community, and closing (30 seconds)**

We build in safety from day one: gas-tight covers tested quarterly with flaring for surplus methane, confined-space gas meters and harnesses for operators against hydrogen sulphide, tetanus and leptospirosis vaccination, and drip trays on the tanker. Shed owners keep defendable permits and save fuel; farmers get free soil amendment; and barangay monitors run the daily verification.

Thank you. We are ready for your questions.

## Part 2. Defense Q&A: Ready Answers for Tough Questions

| Judge's question | Direct answer to give |
|---|---|
| **Why design for 15 sheds (180 head) when you cite a 10,000-head farm in Concepcion?** | They are different populations. The 10,000-head farm sits 500 metres from the town centre and proves why closure orders alone failed. Our pilot is sized to the **small commercial sheds along this 1-km study reach**. If Month 1's MENRO census finds **30 sheds** on the reach, three more digester bays and a second tanker add **₱2.6 million**, which we handle by adjusting scope before construction starts. |
| **What if Month 1 sampling shows households—not piggeries—cause most of the coliform?** | That is built into our Month 1 decision gate. We sample above and below every piggery cluster first. If fecal coliform does not spike below the sheds, the diagnosis shifts explicitly to household septage, and the LGU redirects funds before building the digesters in Month 4. |
| **Why not build a sewage treatment plant at the bridge instead?** | Neither Santiago nor Alfonso has a piped sewer network. Building pipes plus a plant would swallow the entire ₱10 million budget before treating a litre of waste. Truck collection treats high-strength slurry at the source with zero trenching. |
| **Isn't biogas digester technology already decades old?** | Yes—and we state that openly. Standard covered-lagoon equipment is an advantage because operators can maintain it locally without imported parts. Our novelty is institutional: linking the **Mayor's permit renewal** to a **countersigned haulage manifest**, closing the gap seen in Naga City where digesters were mandated on paper without off-farm haulage. |
| **Why assume ₱0 revenue from compost?** | An untested compost market is the easiest way to fake project sustainability on paper. Giving compost to nearby rice farmers in Year 1 secures off-take, avoids stockpiling, and keeps the ₱10 million budget honest. |
| **Why do you mention Bamban's piggeries if you aren't sure their runoff reaches the Lucong?** | We state clearly that cross-boundary drainage into this reach is **untested**. We cite Bamban's land use plan for two factual reasons: first, it documents that commercial piggeries cluster nearby (14 in Bamban, 4 in Anupul); second, Bamban's 2026 zoning ordinance gives Tarlac a ready regulatory template—25 m from groundwater, 500 m from highways, 1,000 m from built-up areas—that can pair with our service model regionally. |
| **Why is your Month 24 target 2,000 MPN/100 mL instead of full 200 compliance?** | Because 16,000 to 2,000 is already an **88 per cent reduction**, and household septic tanks and stormwater still contribute background fecal load. Claiming instant 200 compliance in 24 months would be unrealistic; 200 follows by Month 36 only if the piggery load dominates and parallel household sanitation advances. |
| **What are your biggest remaining data gaps before implementation?** | Three items, all scheduled before Month 4 construction: **(1)** MENRO's exact shed census along the reach, **(2)** EMB's exact station metadata and sample count behind the 16,000 reading, and **(3)** formal supplier quotations for the 2,000-L vacuum tanker, which is our single largest capital line at ₱1.45 million. |

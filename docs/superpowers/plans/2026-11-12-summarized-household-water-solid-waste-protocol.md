# Summarized Plan: Household Waste + Household Water Protocol Pilot

> **⚠ SUPERSEDED 2026-11-13.** Deep research overturned the premise on which this plan was built. Solid waste is not the dominant oxygen-demand pollutant in the reach — septage is — and verification-first waste accounting already operates in six Philippine LGUs. The live angle is now **"The Last Meter"**, an access-constrained septage pilot. See [`2026-11-13-paper-draft-v0.1.md`](../../drafts/2026-11-13-paper-draft-v0.1.md) for the decision record and [`2026-11-13-protocol-pack-P1-P6.md`](2026-11-13-protocol-pack-P1-P6.md) for the operating protocols. This file is retained only as the record of the earlier option comparison. Its Stream 1 service architecture, safety rules, legal cautions and gate discipline still apply.

> **Status:** decision summary, not authorization. Fieldwork, procurement, agency contact, spending and submission all require separate human authorization and accountable professional supervision. This condenses [the implementation plan](2026-11-11-waterway-pilot-implementation.md) and [the research dossier](../../../DEEP_RESEARCH_RF2027_WATERWAY_FEASIBILITY.md); where they conflict, the long plan governs.

**One-line direction:** Run a **protocol-first, asset-light service pilot** inside one contiguous 1-km estero reach, where **household solid waste is the primary stream (≥₱7M)** and **household water management is a supporting non-treatment protocol track** inside the remaining ₱3M.

## 1. The approach we are choosing

Three candidate protocol architectures were compared. The scores are planning judgments, not findings.

| | A. Solid waste only | B. Waste + wastewater treatment | **C. Solid waste primary + household water protocol** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Technical evidence | 5 | 3 | 4 |
| Implementability now | 4 | 1 | 4 |
| Avoids new hazards | 5 | 2 | 4 |
| Measurable in 12 months | 4 | 3 | 4 |
| Budget resilience | 5 | 2 | 4 |
| Competition fit / novelty | 2 | 3 | **4** |
| **Weighted (max 100)** | **80** | **45** | **76** |

**Recommendation: C.** It keeps A's auditability, adds a real household-level water component that costs little and needs no permits, and avoids B's fatal problem — B is a wastewater treatment/discharge scheme under RA 9275 and cannot be delivered, permitted or defended within this budget or one year.

**Why C is not "two streams":** the water track does **not** treat, divert or discharge wastewater. It is containment, safe storage, referral and observed-practice protocol. All primary-stream money and the environmental outcome stay with solid waste. Whether the water items count inside the 71.15% primary attribution must be **confirmed in writing with the organizers** — that is an accounting question, not a technical one.

## 2. Scope

**In scope**
- Household source segregation, safe storage, and dependable scheduled collection within one approved 1-km footprint (scenario: 400 households — a costing assumption, not a census).
- Lawful covered transfer points, contracted hauling, verified receiving-facility delivery with receipts.
- Household water protocol: sealed storage of used oil, chemicals, sachets and other non-ordinary liquids; containment of washwater/leachate so nothing enters the estero, street or storm drain; safe drinking-water storage hygiene; written referral and escalation for septic desludging and illegal connections.
- Bank-based observation only.

### 2b. Product track: sensor/valve as a verification instrument (30% secondary)

The team holds a sensor/monitor or automated-valve product. **It is placed in the 30% secondary bucket, not the 70% primary.** The kit names this product class explicitly under Stream 2 ("digital water quality monitors, automated drain shut-off valves"), and page 8 permits combining approaches.

**Role it must play:** the sensor is the **attribution instrument**, not the intervention. Its job is to protect the primary stream's causal claim — by measuring the boundary load continuously, it shows whether household sources actually dominate, which is the weakest link in the whole proposal.

**Three hard rules**

1. **A low-cost sensor is screening, never regulatory evidence.** Enforcement or violation findings require a grab sample, a recognized method and an accredited laboratory with chain of custody. The dossier already states this; a proposal that issues findings from sensor readings alone fails on Practicality and creates legal exposure.
2. **No field-installed automated drain valve in Year 1.** An automated shut-off in a public drain is an obstruction hazard of the same class documented by Honingh et al., carries flood liability, and requires the asset owner's consent (DPWH/MMDA/LGU). Restrict the valve to a **bench demonstration plus written SOP**, or to a single consented private premises with the owner's written authority.
3. **Every alert must be dispositioned.** An alert with no documented action is a device, not a protocol. Publish the false-positive rate.

**The defensible novelty claim is verification, not monitoring.** River water-quality telemetry already exists in the Philippines. What is uncommon is using continuous sensing to **verify service integrity** — confirming that no leachate or contaminated washwater from the transfer points reaches the drain or estero, and that measured load changes track the collection schedule. Claim that, not "we have sensors."

**Out of scope (deliberately)**
- No in-channel structure, rack, boom, dredging, aeration, artificial wetland, treatment plant or drain closure.
- No wastewater treatment, diversion or discharge; no discharge-permit pathway.
- No student in-water or drain entry, confined-space work, sediment disturbance, sharps or hazardous-waste sorting.
- No app, IoT network, sensor dashboard or AI model.
- No displacement, eviction or construction inside the Water Code 3-m easement.

## 3. Five protocol packages (this is the deliverable architecture)

Each package is a short written SOP with the same six fields: purpose, owner, trigger, steps, record, acceptance test + failure response.

| # | Protocol | Owner | Core control | Acceptance test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **P1** | Household waste protocol | Service supervisor | Segregation at source, sealed storage, fixed collection window, no re-mixing in hauling | 100% of enrolled households issued correctly specified kits; zero observed stream re-mixing |
| **P2** | Household water protocol | Community liaison + supervisor | Containment of washwater/leachate and non-ordinary liquids; safe storage; referral list for desludging and illegal connections | Zero observed washwater/leachate discharge to drain or estero; referral log with dated outcomes |
| **P3** | Operator / chain-of-custody protocol | Operations lead + receiving operator | Route, manifest, weighing, receipts, rejected-load and spill response, backup destination | Dry-run test load traced source→destination; simulated rejection resolved lawfully |
| **P4** | Safety and community protocol | Safety lead | Task-specific free PPE for covered workers, washing facilities, incident reporting, stop-work authority, coded household records | Crew trained and equipped; stop-work exercised; no unprotected handling |
| **P5** | Measurement protocol | Independent evaluation lead | Frozen outcome, stations, schedule, exclusions, QA, analysis, decision rule | Protocol signed and dated **before** intervention starts |

P1–P4 are operational documents the team must actually be able to run. P5 is frozen before service begins so results cannot be retuned afterwards.

**Track B2 detail:** six multi-parameter nodes (DO, pH, turbidity, conductivity, temperature) 6 × ₱45,000 = 270,000 · telemetry and connectivity 12 × ₱8,000 = 96,000 · calibration, membranes, reagents and spares = 64,000 · consented mounting and anti-theft enclosures = 40,000 · open-source data platform, no custom app = 20,000 · bench-scale valve demonstration and SOP = 40,000 · confirmatory resampling reserve for alerts = 20,000.

**Budget warning — this is the tightest part of the plan.** The 70% floor leaves only **₱115,000** of headroom in the primary stream, so the sensor program cannot be funded from Track A. It comes out of the 30% bucket, which pushes contingency to **₱650,000 (6.5%)**. A 20% overrun on direct service alone costs ₱1,423,000, so contingency no longer covers a major shock. Either accept and disclose this, or cut the sensor program to four nodes; do not pad the primary stream to manufacture headroom.

**Track A is unchanged** from the long plan: kits 800,000 · transfer points 600,000 · handcarts 200,000 · PPE 150,000 · six workers 1,755,000 · supervisor 540,000 · hauling 1,800,000 · training 240,000 · maintenance 300,000 · backup 400,000 · hygiene 150,000 · chain audits 180,000.

**Rules that do not move:** direct primary spending alone exceeds ₱7M, so overhead and reserve are never used to reach the threshold. All rates are allowances, not quotations. Recyclables revenue is ₱0. Recurring cost is ≈₱500,500/month and needs a named budget holder before handover.

## 6. What we measure (frozen before intervention)

| Measure | Definition | Threshold |
|---|---|---|
| Service reliability | Completed pickup opportunities ÷ scheduled opportunities | ≥95% (M4–M12) |
| Missed-service recovery | Missed opportunities resolved within 24 h ÷ all misses | ≥90% |
| Documented disposition | Mass with valid receiving evidence ÷ total mass transferred | 100% traceable |
| Storage overflow / spill | Recorded uncontrolled events | Target zero, each triggers action |
| Water protocol adoption | Households with functioning containment and safe storage ÷ enrolled | Proposed, set from baseline |
| Washwater/leachate discharge | Observed discharges to drain or estero at audited points | Target zero |
| **Primary environmental** | Standardized downstream-direction visible floating-litter passage rate at fixed boundary | Provisional 20% reduction, only if baseline precision and controllable-source share justify it |
| Safety | Serious incidents and unresolved unsafe conditions | No serious preventable injury |

**Decision rule:** supported target if the 95% interval (temporal dependence accounted for) has a lower bound ≥ T; evidence against T if the upper bound < T; otherwise inconclusive. **A result below T is not "no effect."** No claim of disease prevention, bathing/drinking safety, metal removal, wastewater compliance or full river restoration is ever made from a litter result.

**Product-specific criteria to hold the line on**

1. **Uptime ≥90% valid readings** — published, not assumed. Fouling, silt, theft and power are the real failure modes in an estero.
2. **100% of alerts dispositioned**, with the false-positive rate published.
3. **Non-regulatory status stated in the text.** Screening triggers confirmation; it never issues a finding.
4. **Local repairability** — calibration consumables and spares that a barangay or LGU can buy.
5. **Cost per node and cost per verified event**, both shown.
6. **The "so what" test** — each sensor must change a decision. If a reading changes nothing, delete the node.
7. **Data governance** — open by default, no household personal data, no identifiable imagery.
8. **Consent for every mounting point**, in writing, from the asset owner.

## 7. Hard limits and honest weaknesses

- **Duplication risk.** Paco has an existing estero-management history. If reconnaissance finds no real service gap, reject the site or re-scope — do not rebrand routine cleanup.
- **Upstream dominance.** If most litter arrives from upstream, the 20% target is unreachable even with perfect crews. Diagnose before promising.
- **Sewage, not trash.** Paco's historical BOD is septic-related. Collecting solid waste does not fix it; the water track is referral and containment, not treatment.
- **Novelty is the weakest criterion.** Reliable service is not a national first, and river telemetry already exists locally. The only defensible claim is verification-sensing — using the instrument to prove the service did not leak. A sensor that changes no decision is a deduction, not a feature.
- **The sensor cannot carry the environmental result.** Monitors produce information; the 20% litter target must still come from the 70% primary stream. Judges will ask what the sensor changed.
- **AI provenance.** The kit's current wording excludes AI-generated content, ideas and theories. This work is substantive assistance. Rewriting does not erase provenance.

## 9. One-page version

> We propose a **two-track, one-stream protocol pilot**: household solid waste is the funded primary stream with dependable segregation-to-verified-disposal service; household water management rides along as a low-cost containment, safe-storage and referral protocol that never treats or discharges wastewater. Five written protocols (household waste, household water, chain of custody, safety, measurement) are the actual product. Six gates — eligibility, site, capacity and destination, affordability and permits, frozen baseline, commission — must pass before anyone buys equipment or hires a crew. Success is measured as service reliability first and a bounded litter-passage effect second, reported with uncertainty and with negative results left intact. Nothing here authorizes fieldwork, spending or a competition entry.

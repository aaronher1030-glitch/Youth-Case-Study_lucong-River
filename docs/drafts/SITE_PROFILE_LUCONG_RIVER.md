## 4. Root-cause diagnosis (DPSIR)

### 4.1 Framework

The diagnosis follows the chain **Drivers (why) - Pressures (what enters the river) - State (what the monitoring measures) - Impacts (who and what is harmed) - Responses (what is being done and what is missing)**. The verdict in 4.6 identifies the main driver and the stream it belongs to, and states the test that could falsify it.

### 4.2 Drivers and pressures

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Backyard and small commercial piggeries operate without treatment or regular inspection | Manure and washwater | Drains, bank seepage, direct discharge | EMB reports livestock farms discharging sewage into the Lucong; fecal coliform 16,000 MPN/100 mL | Team note + EMB; piggery count pending (P8) |
| No sewer network; desludging not scheduled | Septic overflow and seepage | Drains and groundwater | No sewer in the LGU service list; EMB cites domestic wastewater | Verified absence of sewer; septic survey pending (P4) |

### 4.6 Verdict

**Main driver: unmonitored commercial runoff, specifically piggeries and livestock operations.** The reasons, in order of weight:

1. EMB's own report states that livestock farms were discharging sewage into the Lucong (EMB Region III, 2024).
2. The chemistry fits fecal loading, not industrial effluent: coliform 80 times the limit with BOD and DO inside limits.
3. Piggeries and shops sit near the reach, and no inspection or permit inventory covers them.

The counterargument stands: septic tanks alone could produce the same coliform signal. Month 1 is designed to separate the two. The team will sample above and below each piggery cluster and each drain; if fecal coliform does not rise below the clusters, the diagnosis shifts to household sewerage. That is a live alternative, not a dismissed one.

Scored against the kit's three drivers:

| Kit driver | Verdict | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Uncollected solid waste | Contributing, secondary | Medium |
| Inadequate sewerage | Major contributor | High for fecal load; apportionment pending |
| Unmonitored commercial or industrial runoff | Main cause | Medium until the outfall and piggery inventory closes P7 and P8 |

Stream selection: **commercial and industrial**, specifically commercial livestock operations. The reach has shops, piggeries and a highway viaduct, but no factories or processing plants. The proposal states this narrow reading rather than claiming a heavy-industrial source.

### 2. Target Site Profile and Root-Cause Diagnosis

The Lucong River crosses Concepcion, Tarlac and irrigates the town's rice and sugarcane fields before it joins the Chico River (Municipal Government of Concepcion, 2024). The study reach runs 1.0 km downstream from Santiago Bridge (15.3300 N, 120.6284 E), the site of an EMB sampling station, to 15.3334 N, 120.6367 E. The full bridge-to-bridge reach measures 1.52 km (Figure 1). Both banks lie in Barangays Santiago and Alfonso, two of the municipality's seven urban barangays, with 15,220 residents and 3,148 households between them (PhilAtlas, 2026).

The reach meets the competition's site selection conditions. DENR's River for Life program listed the Lucong among Tarlac's pollution-vulnerable water bodies (Punto! Central Luzon, 2019). Fecal coliform at the EMB station reached 16,000 MPN/100 mL in the October 2024 to 2025 round, 80 times the 200 MPN/100 mL standard, while BOD (5 mg/L), DO (6.7 mg/L), pH (7.83) and temperature (30.4 degrees Celsius) stayed within DAO 2016-08 limits (Table 2). The MDRRMO flood watch at the bridge read 3.7 m on 22 July 2025 (MDRRMO Concepcion Tarlac, 2025). The site sits inside the team's home municipality.

The pressures come from how the banks are used. Housing and roadside shops crowd both bridge approaches, and paddies and farm lots sit behind the levees. Neither barangay has a sewer network, so households depend on septic tanks. Collection trucks run fixed routes, yet plastic and food packaging still gather below Santiago Bridge (team photographs, November 2025). Road drains and commercial lots empty into the channel, and piggeries operate near the reach.

EMB attributes the coliform exceedance to domestic wastewater and agricultural runoff, and reports livestock farms discharging sewage into the Lucong (EMB Region III, 2024). The chemistry agrees. BOD and DO within limits mean the load lacks the oxygen demand of industrial effluent; the signal is fecal. The team therefore reads unmonitored commercial runoff, mainly piggeries, as the main driver, with inadequate sewerage close behind and uncollected solid waste adding to the load. Of the kit's three streams, the evidence points to commercial and industrial; the reach has no heavy industry.

Month 1 will test that reading. The team will walk the reach with MENRO, GPS-tag every drain and piggery, and sample above and below each cluster. Three 100 square metre bank quadrats will measure the visible waste. If fecal coliform does not rise below the piggery clusters, the diagnosis shifts to household sewerage. MENRO's outfall inventory, collection routes and inspection records will replace the placeholders this proposal flags.

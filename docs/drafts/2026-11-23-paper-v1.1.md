## Abstract

The Lucong River at Concepcion, Tarlac carries 16,000 MPN/100 mL of fecal coliform, eighty times the DAO 2016-08 limit, while BOD, DO, pH and temperature all pass. That signature points to fecal loading without industrial oxygen demand. EMB Region III attributes the load to domestic wastewater and agricultural runoff and reports livestock farms discharging into the river; piggeries stand within metres of both banks. In 2017 the municipality ordered a 10,000-head piggery closed, the provincial board voided the order, and the manure stayed. This paper proposes a shed-level service, from sealed sump to compost, and a load-balance model that connects 225 heads to the monitoring station. The model predicts station coliform falls by s·c, where s is the sheds' share of the load and c the fraction captured. Because s depends on a discharge nobody has measured, the paper reports a conditional target, not a promise. Inside ₱10 million and twelve months, the least-cost feasible design serves 15 to 30 sheds at ₱8,600 to ₱16,800 per shed-month, levelised.

## 1. Introduction to the Proposed Approach

**The site.** The Lucong drains the rice and sugarcane fields of Concepcion before joining the Chico River (Municipal Government of Concepcion, 2024). The study reach runs 1.0 km downstream from Santiago Bridge (15.3300° N, 120.6284° E), where EMB maintains a sampling station, to 15.3334° N, 120.6367° E; the bridge-to-bridge reach measures 1.52 km (Figure 1). Both banks lie in Barangays Santiago and Alfonso, which hold 15,220 residents and 3,148 households (PhilAtlas, 2026). Neither has a sewer, so every home relies on a septic tank.

![Figure 1. The monitored 1-km reach from Santiago Bridge, and the 1.52-km bridge-to-bridge reach to Alfonso Bridge. Zones A and B are built-up banks; zone C is farmland. Base map: OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL); reach measured in Google Earth.](docs/figures/fig1_lucong_site_map_trim.png)

**The failure is specific.** Fecal coliform reached 16,000 MPN/100 mL at the station, eighty times the 200 standard (Table 1). BOD (5 mg/L), DO (6.7 mg/L), pH (7.83) and temperature (30.4 °C) all conform to DAO 2016-08. The neighbouring stations fail differently: Cutcut River exceeds BOD and fails DO. A strong industrial effluent raises BOD and depresses DO, and nothing here does. The signal is fecal, without oxygen demand.

**Table 1.** Water quality at the study site and the worst neighbouring station, October 2024 to 2025 round (EMB Region III, 2024).

| Parameter | Limit | Site | Cutcut |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fecal coliform (MPN/100 mL) | 200 | 16,000 | 170,000 |
| BOD (mg/L) | 7 | 5 | 8 |

| Temperature (°C) | 25 to 31 | 30.4 | 32.4 |

**Diagnosis.** EMB attributes the exceedance to domestic wastewater and agricultural runoff and reports livestock farms discharging sewage into the Lucong (EMB Region III, 2024). Bamban's land use plan, on the southwest boundary, lists 21 livestock and poultry establishments, 14 of them piggeries, four in Anupul, the barangay nearest the head of this channel (Municipality of Bamban, 2026a). Whether their runoff reaches it is untested. The team reads unmonitored commercial runoff, mainly piggeries, as the main driver, with inadequate sewerage close behind and uncollected solid waste adding load.

**Why a service, not a rule.** Concepcion tried the rule. In 2017 the Sangguniang Bayan ordered a 10,000-head piggery 500 m from the town centre closed; the provincial board voided the order, and the zoning ordinance behind it had never been approved (Tarlac Weekender, 2017). The rule failed and the manure stayed. A service that moves the manure answers a failure the ordinance could not.

**What Month 1 measures.** Three bank quadrats of 100 m², MENRO shed records, and paired samples above and below each cluster. Two quantities decide the rest: dry-season discharge at the bridge, and the sheds' share of the load.

## 2. Suggested Process or Product

**The product is a service.** Every piggery on the reach gets a sealed sump and a 200-litre drum, a weekly collection, central digestion, and compost that returns to the farms behind the levees. The manifest is the compliance record; the Mayor's permit condition is the enforcement, attached to the barangay resolution and health certificates the Citizen's Charter already requires (Municipal Government of Concepcion, 2024).

A piped plant needs a sewer that does not exist and cannot be built inside ₱10 million. Truck collection needs no pipe, treats the load before it dilutes, and produces something the farms will take.

**Plan of action, twelve months.** Months 1–2 verify: census the sheds with MENRO, GPS every drain mouth, sample above and below each cluster, secure land consent. Months 3–5 build three covered digesters (two if Month 1 confirms low-volume washing), the composting pad and the paved yard; procure tanker and carts; train two operators and the barangay monitors. Months 6–11 operate a weekly route with one manifest per load and quarterly sampling. Month 12 audit, publish the cost curve, and hand the service to a barangay cooperative or to MENRO.

**Inputs.** At fifteen sheds of fifteen heads, 450 kg of manure and about 5 m³ of washwater a day. Rice husk from local mills supplies the carbon, roughly three parts husk to one part digestate.

**Steps.** Each shed gets a sump and drum, so manure never reaches the bank. A 2,000-litre vacuum tanker runs a fixed weekly route, weighing and logging every load. Slurry enters a 25 m³ covered digester for 10 to 15 days; gas collects under an HDPE cover, heats the pad's water and fires the sheds' scalding tanks. Digestate is dewatered, blended with husk, and composted in rows for 45 days, turned every ten.

**Efficiency.** Of 72 kg of volatile solids entering daily, about half is destroyed, yielding 21 m³ of biogas at 60% methane, or 10 kg of LPG equivalent a day. The compost's nitrogen, 2.2 kg a day, meets the requirement of 6.6 hectares of lowland rice at 120 kg N per hectare. Nothing is discharged; the nutrient that leaves the farms returns to them.

**One design lever.** Volume, not mass, sets the fleet. At 22 litres of washwater per head-day, fifteen sheds need nineteen tanker trips a week; at 10 litres, nine and a half. Low-volume washing is therefore the first design choice the census tests: at 10 litres the same reach fits two digesters instead of three.

## 3. Expected End Output and Its Functions

The end output is an audited service: physical works, a manifest for every load from shed to digester to field, and an unbroken monitoring series at the reach. It removes the load the diagnosis identified. It produces compliance evidence, turning an invisible discharge into a traceable transaction. It produces a cost curve another municipality can copy without repeating the research. And it returns value locally: biogas to the sheds, compost to the farms, and a permit the owners can defend.

The novelty is not the digester. Covered lagoons have run on Philippine farms for decades, and Naga City's 2017 ordinance already requires one on every commercial farm, with no city service to move the manure off it (City Government of Naga, 2017). The novelty is the haulage: a shed keeps its permit only if every load is collected and manifested, so compliance and service become one transaction.

## 4. Feasibility and Economic Analysis

**The model.** One functional unit carries the argument: the station concentration that a given capture rate implies. Let h be heads on the reach, e the fecal coliform a head sheds per day, and Q the dry-season discharge. Then shed load L = h·e, station load L₀ = C₀·Q, the shed share s = L/L₀, and the predicted endpoint

C = C₀ (1 − s·c)

with c the captured fraction. Every term is measured or assumed; none is fitted. The per-head shedding rate is taken as 10⁹ MPN a day, mid-range for swine.

**Table 2.** Predicted station concentration at 15 heads per shed, C₀ = 16,000 MPN/100 mL.

| Q (m³/s) | s | 85% captured | 95% captured |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.02 | 0.81 | 4,900 | 3,600 |
| 0.05 | 0.33 | 11,600 | 11,100 |
| 0.10 | 0.16 | 13,800 | 13,500 |
| 0.20 | 0.08 | 14,900 | 14,800 |

At the lowest plausible flow the sheds carry four-fifths of the load, and the service cuts the station by two-thirds. At the highest they carry eight per cent, and it barely moves the number. **The target is therefore conditional: halve the station to 8,000 MPN/100 mL, provided s ≥ 0.6, which at this census means Q ≤ 0.03 m³/s.** Month 1 measures both. If s falls below 0.6, the service cannot deliver that target alone, and this paper says so before the pilot starts. The 200 standard lies beyond one kilometre of shed service and is not promised.

The total is ₱6.84 million against a ₱10 million ceiling, itemised in Table 4. The balance is held back rather than spent: the census decides whether it buys a second cluster or a second tanker. The unit cost includes capital, which is why it exceeds an operating figure. Sixty sheds is not a larger version of thirty: it needs a second tanker, eight carts and five digesters, and totals ₱11.1 million, above the cap. The budget serves a reach, not a river, and the census decides where the edge falls.

**Who pays.** At fifteen sheds the levelised cost is about ₱1,100 per head-month; at thirty, about ₱570. Whether a shed can carry that is the second thing the census measures. The design assumes the local government carries capital and the sheds carry collection through the permit fee, with compost free in year one. No compost revenue is counted.

**Technical feasibility.** Covered lagoon and fixed-dome digesters are standard Philippine farm equipment, documented in the PCARRD-funded Vigan swine digester (Tacbas et al., 2010). Tankers, pumps and turners are locally fabricated. Operators need trade skills, not a laboratory.

**Why this option.** The alternatives fail on constraints, not preference. A piped plant needs a sewer that does not exist. In-stream structures carry a blockage liability in a flood-watched channel. Enforcement alone has a measured ceiling of zero here, because the ordinance was voided in 2017. Within one kilometre, twelve months and ₱10 million, the service is the only option that can be costed, permitted and measured. That is the sense in which it is best: least cost among feasible designs, not the highest score on a weighted list.

**National scaling.** The same EMB table that condemns the Lucong lists five more failing Tarlac stations. Bamban has ratified a numeric siting schedule: 25 m from groundwater, 500 m from a highway for commercial piggeries, 1,000 m from built-up areas (Municipality of Bamban, 2026b). Tarlac has diagnosed reaches and a regulatory template; what it lacks is the service. The blueprint is one reach, one manifest, one cost curve, then copy.

## 5. Implications

**Environment.** The service removes manure before it reaches the Chico and the Pampanga system. It creates three hazards of its own: methane escaping a poor cover, digestate spread past what fields absorb, and slurry spilled in transit. Covers are pressure-tested quarterly and surplus gas is flared; compost follows a nutrient budget agreed with the Municipal Agriculture Office; the tanker carries drip trays and washes down at a bay that drains back to the digester.

**Health.** Workers carry the real risk: hydrogen sulphide in the headspace, drums lifted by hand, wet concrete underfoot. Entry follows a confined-space protocol with a gas meter, a harness and a second person at the hatch; operators get boots, gloves and vaccination for tetanus and leptospirosis. For the community the direction reverses, because the bank stops receiving raw manure, so exposure at washing and bathing points falls, and the smell with it.

**Community.** Shed owners gain biogas, compost and a defensible permit. Barangay monitors sign the manifests. Farmers receive the compost. The service is run by people who live on the reach, which is the only version of it that survives the grant.

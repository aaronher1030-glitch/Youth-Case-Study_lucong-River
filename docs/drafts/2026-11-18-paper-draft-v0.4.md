## 2. Target Site Profile and Root-Cause Diagnosis

### 2.1 The site

The Lucong River drains Concepcion, Tarlac, and irrigates its rice and sugarcane fields before it joins the Chico River (Municipal Government of Concepcion, 2024). The study reach runs from Santiago Bridge (15.3300 N, 120.6284 E), where EMB maintains a sampling station, 1.0 km downstream to 15.3334 N, 120.6367 E; the full bridge-to-bridge reach measures 1.52 km (Figure 1). Both banks lie in Barangays Santiago and Alfonso, two of the municipality's seven urban barangays. Santiago had 5,686 residents in 2020 and Alfonso 9,534, with 3,148 households between them in 2015 (PhilAtlas, 2026).

The channel runs 40 to 60 m wide at the bridges, a map estimate; depth has not been measured and will be taken at three cross-sections in Month 1. Flow runs west to east, and stages rise during the June-to-October wet season. The MDRRMO keeps a flood watch at Santiago Bridge that read 3.7 m on 22 July 2025 (MDRRMO Concepcion Tarlac, 2025). Three bridges cross the reach. The Concepcion-Capas Road follows the left bank, the Subic-Clark-Tarlac Expressway crosses on viaduct, and a DPWH dike on the left bank was rehabilitated in 2022.

Land use says where the waste can come from. Dense housing and roadside shops line both banks in Santiago and Alfonso; irrigated paddies and small farm lots lie behind the levees, with bamboo and trees along parts of the bank. Neither barangay has a sewer network, so households rely on septic tanks. The municipality collects garbage on scheduled routes, but plastic and food packaging still gathers on the bank below Santiago Bridge, and the team's photographs from November 2025 show turbid gray-green water there. Road drains and commercial lots discharge into the channel, and piggeries operate near the reach.

### 2.2 Why this site

DENR's River for Life program listed the Lucong among Tarlac's water bodies vulnerable to pollution in 2019 (Punto! Central Luzon, 2019). Fecal coliform at the Santiago Bridge station reached 16,000 MPN/100 mL in the October 2024 to 2025 monitoring round, eighty times the 200 MPN/100 mL standard (Table 1). The reach is urban, flood-watched, and inside the team's home municipality. Impact on fisherfolk belongs to the selection brief, but no fisherfolk registry has been obtained, so that part of the case waits on the barangay (P6).

### 2.3 Evidence and methods

The profile separates public records from team observations and says which is which. The EMB regional report supplies the monitoring values, the station list and its account of pollution sources (EMB Region III, 2024). The citizen's charter describes municipal services (Municipal Government of Concepcion, 2024), census figures come from PhilAtlas (2026), and the river level comes from the MDRRMO flood post. Photographs taken in November 2025 and measurements in Google Earth and OpenStreetMap cover what the records omit.

Fourteen checklist items have no data yet. Each is tracked as a placeholder with a named owner and a collection method, from the piggery count (P8, barangay business registry and MENRO) to the water-quality provenance (P12, EMB Region III). Because no outfall inventory exists, the map marks land-use zones instead of discharge points: zone A holds household waste and septic effluent, zone B the shops and backyard piggeries, zone C the farmland behind the levees. OpenStreetMap labels the channel Sapang Kawayan; EMB and LGU records call it the Lucong, and the team uses the official name.

The failure is specific. BOD (5 mg/L), DO (6.7 mg/L), pH (7.83) and temperature (30.4 degrees Celsius) all pass DAO 2016-08. The neighboring stations in the same EMB table fail differently: Cutcut River exceeds BOD at 8 mg/L and fails DO (4.5) and temperature (32.4), and Masalasa Creek fails DO and temperature. The Lucong fails fecal coliform alone. Strong industrial effluent would raise BOD and depress DO, and nothing here does, so the signature narrows the search to sources that shed pathogens without heavy oxygen demand. The table's limits match DAO 2016-08 for Class C fresh water; the river's official classification will be confirmed with EMB before the final paper leans on it (P12).

### 2.7 The main cause and its rival

Piggeries and livestock operations are the main cause the team will test. EMB reports livestock farms discharging sewage into the Lucong (EMB Region III, 2024), sheds stand near both banks, and the fecal-only chemistry fits animal waste. The rival account deserves its own statement: every house on the bank drains to a septic tank, and septic tanks can produce the same coliform signal. Month 1 sampling is built to tell the two apart, with samples taken above and below each piggery cluster and each major drain, and fecal coliform, BOD and nutrients compared across them. If the clusters do not raise the load, the diagnosis moves to household sewerage.

### 2.8 Testing the diagnosis

In Month 1 the team walks the reach with MENRO and GPS-tags every drain mouth and piggery. Water is sampled above and below each cluster. Depth is measured at three cross-sections: below Santiago Bridge, at Lucong Bridge, and 100 m above Alfonso Bridge. Bank waste is sorted by material and weighed in three 100-square-metre quadrats, one below Santiago Bridge, one at Lucong Bridge and one above Alfonso Bridge, so the visible litter becomes kilograms per 100 square metres and a bank-load estimate.

The records still to request close the rest of the gaps: MENRO's outfall and drain inventory, waste collection routes and volumes for the two barangays, establishment inspection records, the barangay business registry for the piggery count, septage desludging records, and the zoning map that settles whether the "Bamban farms" note refers to lots worked by Bamban residents or to a local place name (P14). When these come back, the diagnosis either holds or moves to the sewerage account, and the reach gains its first source-level record in the process.

## 3. Conclusion

The Lucong fails one parameter, fecal coliform, by eighty times, and passes the rest. That signature fits fecal loading, and the land use along the banks gives it three candidate sources: piggeries, septic tanks and bank litter. The records point to the piggeries, and the chemistry cannot yet rule the septic tanks out. Month 1 is designed to separate them, and five neighboring stations in the same EMB table fail the same limit, so the answer will matter beyond one reach. No remedy is proposed until the source is confirmed on the ground.

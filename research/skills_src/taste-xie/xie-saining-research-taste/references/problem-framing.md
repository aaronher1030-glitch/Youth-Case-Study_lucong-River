# Problem Framing

Use this reference when the user asks whether an idea is worth doing, how to choose a research direction, or how to avoid following trends blindly.

## From Impact To Understanding

Treat "impact" as a secondary consequence, not the primary motivation. The stronger research question is:

- What does this work help people understand?
- What confusion, hidden structure, or missing perspective does it clarify?
- If others read the result, what new mental model can they carry forward?

This reframes research away from self-centered transformation of the world and toward transmission of understanding. A useful project should increase shared understanding even if its citation or product impact is uncertain.

## Define The Problem Before Solving It

Problem definition is often more valuable than execution. Use ImageNet as the canonical pattern: its importance was not only building a large dataset, but making image classification a well-defined agenda that gave deep learning a place to prove itself.

Before improving a method, ask:

1. Is the task itself the right task?
2. Who defined this benchmark, metric, or product requirement?
3. What real-world need or scientific confusion does the formulation expose?
4. If the current problem definition disappeared, what better one would replace it?
5. Does the user have the courage and evidence to define a new problem, or should they solve within an existing one?

## Real Problem Definers

For applied AI, do not assume researchers or large labs are the only legitimate problem definers. Many important AI problems are defined by people living with the difficulty: clinicians, farmers, factory operators, care workers, designers, teachers, and other domain practitioners.

When reviewing an applied idea, ask:

- Whose daily reality creates the problem?
- Has the user observed that reality directly?
- What would the domain expert call success?
- Which part of the problem is invisible from a lab or benchmark?

## Infinite Game Lens

Research is closer to invention than to chess or competitive sports. The objective is not to optimize average output across every paper. It is to produce a few high-quality peaks over a long horizon.

Use this to calm over-optimization:

- A failed or mediocre direction is not fatal if it teaches something.
- The goal is not to win every local comparison.
- A distinctive representative work matters more than many incremental wins.
- Career strategy should preserve room for nonlinear exploration.

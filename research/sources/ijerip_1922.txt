ABSTRACT 
The study aimed to develop an integrated ecological management action plan 
by analyzing the environmental behavior of residents in selected barangays of 
Concepcion, Tarlac. Using a quantitative survey approach, it examined attitudes, 
practices, and perceptions related to waste management, energy use, water 
conservation, and other green practices. The study involved 150 respondents 
from five purposively selected barangays in Concepcion, Tarlac—Alfonso, 
Café, San Francisco, Sta. Rosa, and Santiago—to capture diverse community 
contexts. Each barangay contributed 30 participants, comprising 20 residents 
and 10 barangay officials, to represent both public and leadership perspectives 
on environmental management. Convenience sampling was used, selecting 
participants based on their availability and willingness to participate. Results 
revealed a generally high level of environmental awareness, particularly in solid waste management, influenced 
by local campaigns and environmental experiences. However, enforcement of environmental laws and program 
implementation varied across communities due to resource limitations and leadership differences. The study 
emphasized the need for stronger policy enforcement, continuous capacity building, and active community 
participation to enhance sustainable environmental management. 
Keywords: Environment, awareness, behavior, practices, ecological management plan 
 
INTRODUCTION 
Concepcion, Tarlac stands at a critical crossroads where rapid development intersects with environmental 
sustainability. As one of the fast-growing municipalities in the Philippines, it is endowed with rich natural 
resources and biodiversity that support both local livelihoods and ecological balance. However, the accelerating 
pace of urbanization, population growth, and industrial expansion has placed unprecedented pressure on its 
environment. Pollution, deforestation, and improper waste management are no longer isolated problems but 
growing threats that compromise public health, food security, and the long-term viability of the community. These 
challenges raise an urgent and compelling question: are the residents of Concepcion sufficiently aware of these 
environmental issues, and are they actively practicing behaviors that support ecological protection? Understanding 
this human dimension of environmental sustainability is essential because environmental policies and programs 
can only succeed when the people who live within the ecosystem recognize their role in protecting it. 
Studies in environmental management consistently emphasize that community awareness and 
participation are among the strongest predictors of successful environmental protection. While local governments 
may enact ordinances and implement conservation programs, these initiatives often fail to achieve their intended 
impact without the active involvement of the community. In Concepcion, despite the existence of environmental

Literature Review 
Over the past two decades, environmental concerns have intensified alongside economic growth, 
particularly in developing countries where increased energy use, consumption, and industrial activity have led to 
worsening environmental conditions. Despite having environmental laws aligned with international standards, 
many developing nations continue to experience pollution, energy waste, and poor solid waste management, which 
negatively affect both local communities and global ecosystems. Improper waste disposal—especially plastics, 
chemicals, and other non-biodegradable materials—contributes to climate change, ocean pollution, and ecosystem 
degradation. Studies show that waste segregation is often hindered by high costs, lack of facilities, and limited 
public awareness (Kihila, 2021). 
The Philippines is among the most climate-vulnerable countries, facing risks such as extreme heat, 
droughts, floods, and water contamination. While residents generally value environmental protection, awareness 
alone has proven insufficient to drive sustained pro-environmental behavior. Research highlights the need for 
stronger government commitment, empowered local communities, and continuous, experience-based 
environmental education to promote long-term ecological responsibility (Corpus et al., 2023; Bollettino et al., 
2020). Economic growth, while essential for development, often conflicts with environmental sustainability due 
to increased resource use and emissions, contributing to global warming (Kandel, 2020; Ginez & Tabag, 2023). 
Local information and education campaigns have shown only moderate success in improving climate awareness 
(Vargas et al., 2016). Effective environmental governance depends on meaningful community engagement, cross-
sector collaboration, and well-trained enforcement personnel. However, limited manpower, insufficient training, 
and weak implementation of environmental laws remain persistent challenges (Barreda, 2022). Environmental 
education—both formal and informal—is critical for transforming awareness into informed action, yet some 
communities lack sustained IEC programs and rely only on basic reminders for waste management (Almazan & 
Vargas, 2016). Combining traditional and digital engagement strategies has been shown to improve public 
participation (Ramos, 2025). 
The United Nations Sustainable Development Goals provide a framework for addressing environmental 
behavior, particularly goals related to responsible consumption, climate action, and clean water and sanitation. 
Household waste management, resource conservation, and participation in environmental activities directly 
contribute to these goals and are especially relevant at the community level (United Nations, 2015). National and 
local studies reveal ongoing issues in solid waste management, air pollution, land degradation, and water 
contamination. Poor waste segregation, increasing waste generation, air pollution–related health risks, soil 
erosion, deforestation, flooding, and inadequate wastewater treatment continue to threaten environmental and 
public health. These findings underscore the urgent need for strengthened environmental education, improved

METHODS 
Research Design 
The researcher used a Descriptive comparative research design. According to Siedlecki (2020). It is a type 
of quantitative research method in which two or more groups are described and compared using specific variables 
without changing any of them. It seeks to identify similarities and differences between pre-existing groups to 
better understand the characteristics or behaviors under consideration. A quantitative research strategy would 
involve collecting and evaluating numerical data methodically. According to Bhandari (2020) quantitative 
research is the procedure of gathering and performing a numerical analysis. It may be used to identify trends and 
averages, formulate hypotheses, examine cause and effect, and apply findings to larger groups.  
Research Locale and Sampling Procedures 
 
The study involved 150 respondents from five selected barangays in Concepcion, Tarlac: Barangay 
Alfonso, Café, San Francisco, Sta. Rosa, and Santiago. These barangays were purposively chosen to represent 
diverse community contexts and because they have existing ecological management plans. Each barangay 
contributed 30 respondents, composed of 20 residents and 10 barangay officials, to capture both community 
practices and leadership perspectives on environmental management. Convenience sampling was used based on 
participant availability and willingness. Eligible respondents included residents aged 18 and above and active 
barangay officials involved in governance, while minors, temporary residents, and non-participating individuals 
were excluded. 
Research Instrument 
To collect the data for this study, the researcher used a survey questionnaire created after reading several 
relevant studies. The first ten (10)-item statements on the questionnaire assessed respondents' knowledge of 
environmental concepts and states, while the following ten (10)-item statements addressed ecological issues and 
problems. The third section, which has ten (10) items, assesses their use of environmental practices to solve 
environmental issues. The final section, which also consists of ten items, discussed the level of knowledge, 
training, and cooperation of the local authorities with other agencies. 
Data Gathering Procedures 
The researcher secured permission from the barangay offices of Alfonso, Café, Sta. Rosa, Santiago, and 
San Francisco to collect data. The questionnaire was tested for reliability with a statistician and validated by three 
experts. Informed consent was obtained from all respondents, and coordination with barangay secretaries was 
conducted to schedule the questionnaire administration. 
Data Analysis 
 
The study used descriptive statistics to summarize the data. Median scores were interpreted as follows: 4 
– Very Much Aware/Always/Fully Implemented; 3 – Much Aware/Often/Implemented; 2 – Slightly 
Aware/Sometimes/Partially Implemented; and 1 – Not Aware/Seldom/Not Implemented.

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION 
Table 1. Level of Awareness to Environmental Concepts and the State of Environment in terms of Climate 
Change 
Table 1 shows that residents of Concepcion, Tarlac are generally aware of climate change, with an overall 
median score of 3. The highest awareness was on proper solid waste management (median = 4), while other 
indicators scored 3, indicating broad understanding of climate issues. Despite this knowledge, participation in 
environmental programs and adoption of renewable energy remain limited due to financial and institutional 
constraints. The findings highlight the need for stronger local support, better policy enforcement, and coordinated 
community initiatives to translate awareness into sustained environmental action, in line with the Climate Change 
Act of 2009 (RA 9729). 
Table 2. Level of Awareness to Environmental Concepts and the State of Environment in terms of Global 
Warming 
Table 2 shows that residents of Concepcion, Tarlac are generally aware of global warming and barangay-

residents recognize these efforts, weak enforcement and limited sustainability hinder their effectiveness. The 
findings indicate support for barangay programs, but inconsistent implementation and lack of long-term planning 
limit impact. Strengthening education, monitoring, and program continuity is needed to translate awareness into 
sustained environmental action.

CONCLUSION 
Residents of Concepcion, Tarlac demonstrate strong environmental awareness, particularly in solid waste 
management, influenced by personal experience and MENRO’s IEC campaigns. Daily practices like waste 
segregation and energy conservation reflect individual responsibility, though barangay-level enforcement and 
program consistency vary. Awareness of climate change, global warming, and deforestation is generally uniform 
across barangays, yet knowledge alone does not ensure action. Environmental understanding of air, water, land, 
and noise pollution differs by barangay, shaped by exposure, leadership, and local initiatives such as clean-up 
drives and noise regulation enforcement. While household waste management is common, public compliance and 
broader sustainable practices like tree planting are uneven, affected by infrastructure, population, and policy 
support. Implementation of environmental laws, including RA 9003 (Solid Waste Management) and RA 8749 
(Clean Air Act), is strongest, whereas RA 9275, RA 9512, and PD 1586 show moderate execution due to limited 
local engagement. Training disparities among barangay officials and gaps in resources and coordination further 
influence governance effectiveness. Overall, consistent policy enforcement, targeted education, inter-agency 
coordination, and community involvement are essential to translate awareness into sustained environmental 
action. 
Recommendations 
1. Conduct awareness campaigns on local and national environmental issues through flyers, brochures, and 
symposiums led by barangay officials. 
2. Allocate budgets for trainings, seminars, quarterly tree planting and clean-up drives, and distribution of eco-
friendly materials such as reusable bags and bottles. 
3. Distribute IEC materials detailing environmental ordinances, sanctions, fines, and penalties to inform 
residents.

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
To deliver real time science-based air quality 
monitoring data available to everyone, the 
installation of Real Time Continuous Ambient 
Air Quality Monitoring Stations capable 
of monitoring complete criteria pollutants 
(SO2, NO2, O3, CO, BTX, and PM10/2.5) 
and meteorological parameters are being 
implemented by making the results available 
on the website, anybody can see the status 
of air quality in their location and elsewhere.
The website is also equipped with layman 
description relating to the concentration of air 
pollutant which enables non-scientific person 
to appreciate the information and apply it 
to their everyday lives. Partnership with host 
institutions/agencies will be encouraged 
for the maintenance and security of the 
equipment. Full operationalization of airshed 
nationwide and implementation of its action 
plan were also being undertaken.
The water quality programs aim to improve 
water quality of priority rivers and other critical 
water bodies including lakes and bays through 
continuous massive clean-up, monitoring 
of industries and rehabilitation of esteros/
rivers through partnership agreements 
such as the adopt-an-estero program. 
Water classification is a very important 
component of water quality management 
since the application of effluent standards 
are dependent on this classification. The 
classification of waterbodies helps water 
managers and planners to develop proper 
water quality management programs and 
provide the standards to protect aquatic 
life and human use of specific water 
bodies. Operationalization of water quality 
management areas (WQMAs) is also 
prioritized.
Manila Bay, however, is still a serious 
concern because of its high fecal coliform 
level. Hence, the Supreme Court issued a 
continuing mandamus to the DENR, other 
concerned government agencies and LGUs 
to clean, rehabilitate and restore Manila Bay 
as well as to restore its water quality to Class 
“SB” level that is fit for swimming, skin diving 
and other forms of contact recreation.
The ecological solid waste management 
program is expected to assist Local 
Government Units in implementing RA 9003 
or the Ecological Solid Waste Management 
Act particularly in the development of their 
10 yr. SWM Plan, closure and rehabilitation 
of dumpsites, establishment of Materials 
Recovery Facilities and an environmentally 
sound disposal system.
The issuance of guidelines and provisions of 
technical support in the establishment and 
operation of Waste to Energy facilities will 
enhance the sustainability of disposal system 
for municipal residual wastes in all provinces 
and highly urbanized areas. The significant 
contribution of this project is expected 
to enhance the economic development 
of the country through formalizing the 
waste collection and recycling as well as 
the promotion of job opportunities. It also 
helps the economy to reduce reliance on 
imported fuels and explore the possibility of 
an additional source in power generation.

INTRODUCTION
1.0 INTRODUCTION
On July 10, 1987 by virtue of Executive Order 
No. 192, the Environmental Management 
Bureau (EMB) was created as a Staff Bureau 
of the Department of Environment and 
Natural Resources (DENR). The Bureau is 
a merger of the National Environmental 
Protection Council (NEPC) and the National 
Pollution Control Commission (NPCC), 
which were then tasked to protect, conserve 
and manage the environment.
In July 1999, Republic Act 8749 or the 
Philippine Clean Air Act was promulgated 
and EMB is converted from a staff bureau into 
a line bureau of the DENR. Consequently, 
the 
Environmental 
Management 
and 
Protected Areas Service (EMPAS) of the 
DENR Regional Offices was reorganized 
paving the way to the establishment/
operationalization of EMB Regional Offices 
in January 2000.
The conversion of the EMB into a line bureau 
and the establishment of the EMB Regional 
Offices put the EMB into a better position to 
strengthen and improve the implementation 
of the following environmental laws: 
Environmental Impact Statement System 
Law (PD 1586), Toxic Substances and 
Hazardous and Nuclear Wastes Control Act 
of 1990 (Republic Act No. 6969), Philippine 
Clean Air Act of 1999 (Republic Act 8749), 
Ecological Solid Waste Management Act 
of 2000 (RA 9003), Philippine Clean Water 
Act of 2004 (Republic Act No. 9275) and 
the National Environmental Awareness and 
Education Act of 2008 (RA 9512).
All 
EMB 
Regional 
Offices 
likewise 
implement all policies, plans, programs, 
projects and activities relative to the 
prevention and control of pollution as well 
as the management and enhancement of 
the environment.
1.1 VISION AND MISSION
The Bureau’s mission is to restore, protect 
and enhance environmental quality towards 
good public health, environmental integrity 
and economic viability and envisions a 
nation empowered to protect our finite 
natural resources, attuned to the pursuit of 
sustainable development for a clean and 
healthy environment that enhances the 
Filipino quality of life for present and future 
generations.
1.2 MANDATE
The EMB is the primary government agency 
under the Department of Environment and 
Natural Resources (DENR) mandated to 
formulate, integrate, coordinate, supervise 
and implement all policies, plans, programs, 
projects and activities relative to the 
prevention and control of pollution as well 
as the management and enhancement of 
the environment.

1.5 MAJOR THRUSTS
The DENR’s main priority is to improve 
the quality of the environment particularly 
the air and water quality and address the 
solid waste management problems.
CLEAN AIR PROGRAM
For clean air, the priority activities are the 
following: (1) monitoring of the compliance 
of firms/industries; (2)operationalization of 
airshed governing boards; and (3) calibration 
and maintenance of air quality monitoring 
stations.
The program covers the full implementation 
of Clean Air Act. The objective of the 
Clean Air Program is to come up with a 
comprehensive national program to achieve 
and maintain air quality that meets the 
National Air Quality Guidelines for Criteria 
Pollutants throughout the Philippines, 
while minimizing the possible associated 
negative impacts on the country’s economy. 
Thus, in order to effectively monitor and 
regulate all sources of air emissions, the 
Clean Air Program is divided into three 
sub programs: (1) Motor Vehicle Emission 
Management 
Program, 
(2) 
Industrial 
Emission 
Management 
Program, 
(3) 
Roadside ambient and general ambient air 
monitoring. This is to effectively manage the 
three sources of air emission namely mobile, 
stationary, and area sources.
CLEAN WATER PROGRAM
For clean water, our priority activities are the 
following: (1) monitoring of the compliance 
of firms/industries; (2) conduct of classification 
of 
waterbodies; 
and 
(3) 
designation/
operationalization of WQMAs.
The program is implemented based on the 
established environmental laws specifically RA 
9275, the Philippine Clean Water Act (CWA) 
which provides for a comprehensive strategy 
to address the degradation of our waterbodies. 
It mandates the Bureau to formulate and 
implement policies and strengthen partnerships 
with stakeholders to improve water quality in 
all parts of the country. It encourages pollution 
prevention and waste minimization at source 
and provides for the use of economic incentives 
and public disclosure to ensure compliance.

SOLID WASTE MANAGEMENT 
PROGRAM
For solid waste management, our priority 
activities are the following: (1) closure of 
dumpsites; and (2) establishment of Material 
Recovery Facilities (MRFs).
The Ecological Solid Waste Management 
Act or RA 9003 was enacted to effectively 
manage and improve the management of 
solid wastes in the country. RA 9003 provides 
for a systematic, comprehensive and 
ecological waste management program to 
ensure the protection of public health and 
the environment. It mandates the Bureau to 
provide secretariat support to the National 
Solid Waste Management Commission 
in the implementation of the solid waste 
management plans and prescribes policies 
to achieve the objectives of the National 
Ecology Center in charge of information 
dissemination, consultation, education and 
training of various local government units on 
ecological waste management.
2.0 REGIONAL PROFILE
2.1 Geography
Central Luzon, also known as Region III (or 
Region 3), is an administrative division or 
region of the Republic of the Philippines 
comprising of seven (7) provinces. It covers 
the provinces of Bataan, Bulacan, Zambales, 
Pampanga, Tarlac, Nueva Ecija and Aurora, 
each known either for historical, scenic, 
tourist, industrial or agricultural assets.
Central Luzon occupies a major part of a 
floodplain that extends from Manila Bay 
to Lingayen Gulf. The plain is the country’s 
top food basket and a leading center for 
settlement and agri-industrial development. 
The area is naturally provided with a 
protective shield against strong typhoons 
by the Sierra Madre mountain range and 
Zambales mountain range in the Pacific and 
West Philippine seaboards, respectively. At

Sampling Method
Beta attenuation monitoring (BAM) is a widely 
used air monitoring technique employing the 
absorption of beta radiation by solid particles 
extracted from air flow. This technique allows for 
the detection of PM10 and PM2.5, which are 
monitored as standards by most air pollution 
regulatory agencies. 
The main principle is based on a kind of Bouguer 
(Lambert–Beer) law: the amount by which the 
flow of beta radiation (electrons) is attenuated by 
a solid matter is exponentially dependent on its 
mass and not on any other feature (such as density, 
chemical composition or some optical or electrical 
properties) of this matter.


15
So, the air is drawn from outside of the detector 
through an “infinite” (cycling) ribbon made from 
some filtering material so that the particles are 
collected on it.
There are two sources of beta radiation placed 
one before and one after the region where air 
flow passes through the ribbon leaving particles 
on it; and there are also two detectors on the 
opposite side of the ribbon, facing the detectors. 
The sources’ intensity and detectors’ sensitivity 
being the same (or corrected with appropriate 
calibration lookup table), the intensity of beta 
rays detected by one of detectors is compared 
to that of the other. Thus, one can deduce how 
much mass the ribbon has acquired upon being 
exposed to airflow, knowing the drain velocity, 
actual particle mass concentration in air could be 
assessed.

3.3 AIR QUALITY MANAGEMENT
3.3.1	
 Stationary Sources
Stationary sources refer to any building or 
immobile structure, facility or installation 
which emits or may emit any air pollutant, 
and may be defined generally as individual 
points of air emissions (e.g. smokestacks). 
Under RA 8749, all industries classified as a 
stationary source should perform their own 
emissions monitoring and report the results 
to the DENR.
All stationary sources of air emissions must 
comply with National Emission Standards 
for Sources Specific Air Pollution (NESSAP) 
and Ambient Air Quality Standard (AAQS) 
pertaining to the source. Upon compliance 
to standards, the DENR issues a Permit to 
Operate (PTO) to these industries prior to 
their operation.
Based on the grounds stated in Section 
12 of DAO 2013-26, a Notice of Violation 
(NOV) may then be issued to industries 
with previously issued PTOs.  In 2022, we 
were able to monitor a total of 2,894 firms 
on their compliance to the Clean Air Act. A 
total of 808 Notices of Violations were issued 
for violations of the Act while 1,545 Permit to 
Operate (APSCI) were issued to firms with air 
pollution control devices.
3.3.2	
Management of Stationary 
Sources
Stationary source emission inventory is a 
comprehensive table sheet of sources, their 
characteristics, and actual emissions. A source 
large enough and contributes a huge volume 
of emissions can be considered as a point 
source, such as power plant boilers, cement 
manufacturing kilns, crematoriums thermal 
furnace, factory’s chimneys, hotel boilers, etc.
The first step is to identify all sources in the 
Region and classify them into categories 
based on type of industries, product 
characterization and combustion process.
The list of these sources and emission data 
can be obtained from the quarterly Self-
Monitoring Reports submitted by the 
Pollution Control Officer (PCO) of the plant. 
For estimating emission, further process 
information from each source type needs 
to be provided directly for the Emission Test 
Report or inspection.
The data normally require the following: 
industry 
type, 
geographical 
position 
(coordinates), air pollution source and its 
control device, capacity of sources, type 
of fuel used, fuel consumption, annual 
operating hours, equipment year installed, 
height of stacks (and if available: inner 
diameter, volume flow in m3/operating 
hour), and actual emission test result.
The emissions for each source category are 
calculated using emission estimate equation 
and its corresponding emission factors.

3.3.3 Mobile Sources
Mobile sources are any vehicle propelled 
by or through combustion of carbon-
based or other fuel, constructed and 
operated principally for the conveyance of 
persons or the transportation of property or 
goods. In a study, it was noted that major 
atmospheric pollutants such as Particulate 
Matter and NOx come mostly from motor 
vehicle exhaust emissions mainly attributed 
to jeepneys, buses and taxicabs. Motor 
vehicles (MVs) are classified by the LTO for 
registration into seven groups: cars, utility 
vehicles (UV), sports utility vehicles (SUV), 
trucks, buses, motorcycles/tricycles (MC/TC) 
and trailers.
In general, increase in vehicle registrations 
are caused by the increase in population 
especially in urban centers to cater to the 
transport needs of the people. Although 
more national roads and bridges are being 
improved and built through the years, 
main roads remain heavily congested 
particularly during rush hours, prolonging 
vehicle emissions as travel time is increased. 
The increase in purchase and registration 
of motorcycles can be attributed to the 
thought that these MVs are faster, use less 
space and consume less fuel compared to 
cars.
 Those who can afford use cars because 
the mass transit system of the country 
is undeveloped, explaining the steady 
increase in car registration as well. The 
solution to heavy traffic is the improvement 
of the Philippines’ mass transport system 
as less vehicles will be used, thus reducing 
mobile emissions.
The DOTr has implemented and still 
continues to promote rationalization projects 
for public transit, especially for buses and 
jeepneys which dominate the roads in 
terms of numbers. The enduring effect of 
Covid-19 has made it more challenging to 
navigate and manage our public transport 
system, given the need to strike a balance 
between public health and safety against 
the vital economic need of our people for 
mobility and connectivity.
On the other hand, the LTFRB LTO inspects 
motor vehicle emissions as per RA8749. 
Compliance to standards is the requirement 
for new and renewal of registration while 
penalties are imposed to those who exceed 
the limit.
To reduce vehicular emissions, non-
motorized transport (bicycle use) is being 
promoted as well as the use of ‘cleaner 
fuels’ such as liquefied petroleum gas 
(LPG) and compressed natural gas (CNG). 
Hybrid cars and electricity-powered vehicles 
are also gaining more support from the 
government, private and the public sector.
The emission from mobile sources can 


22
be estimated based on the motor vehicle 
activity rates, fuel consumption and emission 
factors. This includes the number of vehicles, 
number of trips per vehicle type per day, 
daily trips per year and average length of

are motorcycles, passenger cars, light-duty 
vehicles, heavy-duty vehicles and buses/
coaches in which fuel includes gasoline, 
diesel, Liquefied Petroleum Gas (LPG) and 
natural gas, if available. Cold start exhaust 
emission, Hot start exhaust emission and 
evaporative must also be considered in 
mobile sources emission inventory.

PUV Modernization Program in Pampanga 
– The Land Transportation Franchising 
and Regulatory Board (LTFRB) is tasked to 
implement the PUV Modernization Program 
(PUVMP) which aims to have a reliable, 
safe, adequate, accessible, environment-
friendly, efficient, and comfortable public 
transportation services. The PUVMP was 
launched in the province of Pampanga in 
2019 by the Department of Transportation 
(DOTr) and LTFRB. There are now 25 various 
transport cooperatives in the region that 
have joined the program.
The PUVMP is not merely a vehicle 
modernization program. It is a comprehensive 
system reform that will entirely change 
the public land transportation industry. It 
features a regulatory reform and sets new 
guidelines for the issuance of franchise for 
road-based public transport services. The 
program devolved the function of route 
planning to the local government units 
as they are more versed in the terrain and 
passenger demand within their respective 
territorial jurisdiction.
The LGUs, on the other hand, are required 
under the program to submit their own 
Local Public Transport Plan (LPTRP) as 
a prerequisite for the opening of PUV 
franchises within their jurisdiction.
The modern PUV units run on environment-
friendly systems, and are equipped for safety 
and travel comfort as it is equipped with 
free wi-fi, USB charge ports, personal fans, 
air conditioner, automatic fare collection 
systems, convenient with due consideration 
to persons with disabilities (PWDs).
Under the PUV modernization program, 
jeepneys look like minibuses but are called 
modernized jeepneys from class 1 to class 3, 
depending on the capacity and size. Some of 
the modernized jeepneys are electric, solar- 
powered, Euro 4, and there is even a Euro 6 
diesel. Benefits of the program include the 
giving of salary to the drivers instead of the 
boundary pay system, fleet management, 
reduced costs, and increased profits through 
a cooperative system among others.
Route rationalization studies will also be 
conducted to determine the appropriate 
mode, quantity and service characteristics of 
the public transport service in each corridor 
which will make the routes more responsive 
to passenger demand, and ensure that 
the hierarchy of roads and modes of 
transportation are followed.
3.3.5 Area Sources
Area source is a source of air emissions 
that is not confined to a discrete point or 
points of emissions, examples of which (but 
not limited to) are construction activities 
(TSP or PM10), unpaved road ways (TSP or 
PM10), lagoons (photochemically reactive 
compounds and/or other emissions),
industrial facilities with many small or 
generalized potential sources such as 
valves, seals, etc. (photochemically reactive 
compounds and/or other emissions); and 
common generally industrial, small, non-


23
regulated point sources (e.g. dry cleaners 
and gasoline stations) where the point 
source(s) cannot feasibly or practically be 
measured. Household cooking, meat curing 
and waste burning are also among the 
contributors to area sources. 
Pursuant to Part VII, Section 13 (d) of RA8749 
IRR, open burning of materials such as 
plastic, polyvinyl chloride, paints, ink, wastes 
containing heavy metals, organic chemicals, 
petroleum related compound, industrial 
wastes, 
ozone 
depleting 
substances 
and other similar toxic and hazardous 
substances, is prohibited.
Source categories are different depending 
on the feature of the study area, and some 
common categories for cities in developing 
countries are biomass (straw) burning, solid 
waste open burning, household cooking, 
and smoke from meat grilling.
3.3.6 Management of Area and Other 
Sources
As specified in Section 24 of RA 8749, there is 
also a ban on smoking inside a public building 
or an enclosed public place including public 
vehicles and other means of transport or in 
any enclosed area outside of one’s private 
residence, private place of work or any duly 
designated enclosed smoking area.
Implementation of this policy seems lacking as 
a large part of the 17.3 million Filipino (aged 
15 years old and above) smokers can be seen 
smoking every day in the streets and public 
vehicles. Alongside the increase in urbanization 
rates and economic development, the 
number of construction activities has also 
increased during the past decade.
In the beginning, a list of all sources 
and their information are collected from 
related local Provincial Office or other 
related Departments, as well as the data 
of inhabitants, the number of households, 
and the density rate in each ward. These 
data are used for calculating emission from 
common sources distributed evenly, such 
as household cooking, using a top-down 
approach by which the total emissions 
for the whole region are calculated, and 
then they are distributed by each grid cell 
according to the number of households and 
the density rate.
Sources with the same characteristic 
are classified under a category, and an 
appropriate emission estimation method 
is chosen depending on the source 
characteristic and the data available. The 
total emissions of one grid include the 
emission from household activities and 
from other sources.

4.0 WATER QUALITY
4.1 SOURCES OF WATER POLLUTION
There are many types of pollutants that can 
deteriorate the quality of water in our rivers and 
streams. It is important to know where these 
pollutants come from because efforts to prevent 
water pollution should start with preventing the 
source of pollutants from discharging compounds 
that can threaten our waterbodies. Sources of water 
pollutants can be classified according to land use 
from where they originated or according to the 
mode of discharge or input of pollutant to the body 
of water. Classification of sources of pollution based 
on land use of origin are: domestic, commercial, 
agricultural and industrial sources.
Domestic sources include households or 
residential areas. Everyday use of water is 
necessary for drinking, cooking, bathing and 
washing purposes hence, pollutants coming 
from these sources are unavoidable. This makes 
domestic sources the primary polluter of our 
rivers. When these used waters are discharged to 
drainage and mixed with other wastes like plastics 
and solid wastes, among others, they become 
even more a threat to the water quality of our 
rivers. These domestic sources may carry with it 
nutrients, organic materials that may degrade in 
water including some toxic chemicals. Drainage 
discharges are carried into the rivers and run-off on 
land as a result of heavy rainfall.
Commercial sources include establishments 
such as food parks, restaurants, private offices, 
stores, supermarkets and other businesses that 
cater to the public. They use a lot of water for 
their everyday operations. Wastewater coming 
from commercial sources carries with it nutrients, 
organic matters and pathogens. Used water from 
restaurants specifically contain fats, oil and grease. 
They also reach waterbodies through drainage 
networks and by run-off especially during rainfall.

Agricultural sources are those coming from 
agricultural lands such as farms and livestock. 
Farmlands use fertilizers which are rich in 
nutrients especially nitrogen and phosphorous 
that are needed for plant growth. Pesticides 
sprayed in crops also contain substances that 
may be toxic at high levels. Animal manure 
from livestock also contain nutrients, organic 
matter and pathogens.
Industrial sources of water pollutants are 
manufacturing plants and other industrial 
facilities. Industrial processes produce different 
types of toxic chemicals such as heavy metals 
and other inorganic substances. Mercury, 
arsenic and cadmium are usually found in water 
coming from mining-related industries while 
cyanide is normally used in industries using 
combustion, in cigarettes and plastics. Lead 
is found in many manufactured commercial 
products including plastics. Nickel is released 
by power plants and combustion plant.
The different type of sources can be further 
categorized into two, according to the mode 
of discharge to the rivers: point and non-point 
sources.
Point sources are those whose point of 
discharge to rivers can be easily located and 
identified. These sources could be a pipe 
discharging used water from residences, 
industries, and commercial establishments. 
Non-point sources are those that cannot 
be easily attributed to a single discharge 
point. Discharge from these sources 
come from a wide area instead of a 
single point. Non-point sources include 
agricultural 
lands 
where 
pesticides 
are applied over the entire area and 
where sediments may be washed from 
anywhere in the land.
Residential areas that pollute rivers by 
throwing into the river can be considered 
non-point sources because the exact 
source of solid waste can be attributed 
to the entire residential area instead 
of a single point of disposal into the 
river. Run-off from residential areas are 
also considered non-point sources of 
pollution.
Over the years, these sources of pollution 
are increasing due to the result of rapid 
urbanization which led to an increasing 
number of residential areas, commercial 
establishments with different types of 
businesses as well as the presence of 
industrial and manufacturing plants.
While we need these developments 
for the economy and the growth of 
the country, it should be coupled with 
proper regulation to control these 
sources of water pollution and minimize, 
if not completely prevent, the effects of 
pollutants in our waterbodies.

Water quality measures how good water is in 
terms of its beneficial use and environmental 
value. It is water relative to its use and 
measured in terms of its physical, chemical, 
biological and radiological characteristics. 
It is most frequently used in reference to a 
set of standards against which compliance 
can be assessed. Wastewater projection 
indicate that most cities and growing 
municipalities have higher water demand 
compared to the other areas in the region. 
These areas are more exposed to problems 
related to water quality and health, among 
them waterborne diseases.
Waterborne 
diseases 
are 
generally 
transmitted 
through 
water 
in 
which 
pathogenic microorganisms live. These 
diseases can be spread while bathing, 
washing, or drinking water, or by eating 
food exposed to contaminated water.

Governing Board (GB) was created. Through its

rehabilitate the river system and transform it into 
an eco-tourism site.
The upstream portion of the Marilao River 
starts from the area of the City of San Jose 
Del Monte passing through Sta. Maria 
and Marilao and has a tributary while the 
upstream portion of the Meycauayan River 
begins in Caloocan City passing through 
Marilao and the City of Meycauayan and 
has a tributary coming from Valenzuela City. 
The two rivers meet onto the Obando River 
with another tributary river from Bocaue 
before draining to the Manila Bay. 
The catchment area of the MMORS has 
numerous settlements and industries that 
are discharging their wastewater into the 
river. Informal settlers are also present along 
the banks and they largely contribute to the 
domestic waste being dumped directly into 
the river. There are also significant industrial 
wastes haphazardly being dumped into the 
Meycauayan River. Other problems that can 
be observed are the presence of solid waste 
and other floating debris, siltation and 
unpleasant odor emanating from the river.
Pursuant to DENR Administrative Order No. 
08, Series of 2016 “Water Quality Guidelines 
and General Effluent Standards of 2016”, 
the three rivers exceeded the Water Quality 
Guidelines for Class C on parameters: 
Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD5) and 
Fecal Coliform. Dissolved Oxygen on all 
rivers is below the WQG for Class C of 5 
mg/L. There is also exceedance in Phosphate 
level on the three rivers, and Chlorides in 
areas of Meycauayan and Obando.
The Marilao-Meycauayan-Obando River 
System continuous to exceed in its level 
of BOD, Fecal Coliform and Phosphates 
based on the WQG pursuant to DAO 
2016-08, and its Dissolved Oxygen remains

continuing problem in domestic wastewater 
and other biological and industrial waste 
sources. The LGUs shall be assisted on 
the implementation of their septage 
management plans. There shall also be 
capacity building for the monitoring of 
industries by the environmental staff of 
the LGUs involved to strengthen the 
enforcement of RA 9275.

5.0 SOLID WASTES
The Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) projected 
that Region 3’s population will be at 12,706,460 
(by July 1, 2022) as projected in the mid-year 
population based on the 2015 population 
census (POPCEN) results. The average waste 
generation of a single person is about 0.5 kg 
of waste per capita per day. This means that 
the estimated waste generation in the region is 
about 6.3 million kilograms per day.
Solid Waste Management remains a major 
environmental issue in most developing 
countries like the Philippines. To take initiative 
in managing these daunting problems on 
ecological solid waste disposal, the Philippine 
legislative bodies enacted  Republic Act No. 
9003 otherwise known as the “Ecological 
Solid Waste Management Act of 2000 on 
January 26, 2001 and came into effect on 
February 16, 2001.
The Act aims to address the gargantuan 
problem on solid wastes in the country. RA 
9003 is an act providing for an ecological 
solid waste management program, creating 
the necessary institutional mechanisms and 
incentives, declaring certain acts prohibited 
and providing penalties, appropriating funds 
therefor, and for other purposes. RA 9003 is 
considered as the most comprehensive solid 
waste management act in the Philippines. 
It 
provides 
the 
necessary 
institutional 
mechanisms with the creation of the National 
Solid Waste Management Commission 
(NSWMC) 
which 
shall 
oversee 
the 
implementation of solid waste management 
plans and prescribe policies as well as 
incentives to achieve objectives of the Act and 
that is to ensure the protection of the public 
health and environment. 
5.1 SOURCES AND CHARACTERISTICS 
OF SOLID WASTE
Waste Characterization and Analysis Study

5.4.3 LEGISLATION AND POLICY 
Extended Producers Responsibility 
Act (EPRA) of 2022
The Extended Producers Responsibility Act 
(EPRA) of 2022 is an Act institutionalizing the 
extended producer responsibility on plastic 
packaging waste, amending Republic Act 
No. 9003, otherwise known as the Ecological 
Solid Waste Management Act of 2000.
The EPRA lapsed into law on 23 July 
2022. It requires large companies to adopt 
and implement policies for the proper 
management of plastic packaging wastes in 
response to the clamor to regulate single-use 
plastics and their production, importation 
and disposal by industries. The law requires 
obliged large enterprises to recover up to 
80% of their plastic packaging waste.
Enforcement and Compliance
Enterprises required to implement the EPR 
program shall within six (6) months following 
the effectivity of the EPR Law, establish or 
phase-in EPR programs for plastic packaging. 
Large enterprises may voluntarily organize 
themselves to form or authorize a Product 
Recovery Organizations  (PRO) for the purpose 
of establishing a viable platform to implement 
their EPR program. Large enterprises or the 
PRO shall register EPR programs with the 
National Solid Waste Commission (NSWC) 
through the DENR.
Recovery actions that prevent waste from 
leaking into the environment include the 
following: Buying back material or waste 
from consumers, putting up collection 
points where the material or waste can 
be dropped off after consumption then 
collected for reuse or recycling, clean-up 
of waste from coastal areas, public roads, 
other places and establishment of recycling, 
compositing, thermal treatment, and other 
waste diversion or disposal facilities.
Targets for the recovery of plastic product 
footprint generated during the  immediately 
preceding year have been set:
31 December 2023 – 20%
31 December 2024 – 40%
31 December 2025 – 50%
31 December 2026 – 60%
31 December 2027 – 70%
31 December 2028 – 80%
Large enterprises or their PROs shall establish 
and implement an auditing system to monitor 
and assess their compliance performance with 
the EPR Law and their EPR programs. For this 
purpose, they shall engage an independent 
third-party auditor to certify the veracity of the 
reported plastic product footprint generation, 
recovery, and EPR program compliance, using 
uniform standards established by the DENR. 
The audited report shall be submitted by the 
obliged enterprises or their PROs to the DENR.
Non-compliant enterprises will have to 
pay twice the recovery and diversion of 
their plastic footprint or its shortfall or the 
appropriate fine whichever is higher. The 
following fines are: Php 5 million to Php 10 
million (first offense), Php 10 million to Php15 
million (second offense) and Php 15 million 
to Php 20 million (third offense). The DENR’s 
Pollution and Adjudication Board will hear 
and judge cases of alleged violations and 
the imposition of fines.

5.6 CHALLENGES 
Twenty-two (22) years after the passage 
of RA 9003, enforcement and compliance 
with the law remains a daunting task due to 
technical, financial and political limitations 
of LGUs and other concerned agencies. In 
Region 3, only a handful of the 130 LGUs 
have yet to comply with the provisions of 
the act, particularly on the submission of 
10 Year SWM Plans and establishment of 
Materials Recovery Facilities. 
While, limitations of the Regional Office 
on R.A. 9003 implementation include the 
inadequate regular personnel focused in 
accomplishing the complex tasks of SWM 
and insufficient budget allocated in attaining 
the goals of the said Act. Along with is 
the limited number of vehicles used for 
monitoring. Acquisition of additional vehicles 
under ESWM Section would be a great help 
in accomplishing the tasks. Accordingly, 
baseline data and other necessary SWM 
information are still unavailable. 
Another issue includes lack of capacity 
building/training in understanding the 
provision of the law, lack of enforcement of 
ordinances, political will and terms of office 
of the local officials whereof government 
officials are afraid to apprehend violators 
for the reason that people may not vote 
for them during the elections.
Furthermore, the non-mandatory position of 
the MENRO and the short term of office which 
in due course affects the continuity of the 
programs and lastly, lack of funds or budget 
and technical assistance needed to access 
credit facilities and to connect to the private 
sector. These issues lead to unsustainable 
implementation of LGUs in RA 9003 which it 
weakens the compliance of LGUs.

6.0 TOXIC CHEMICALS
& HAZARDOUS WASTES
Republic Act (RA) 6969, otherwise known 
as the Toxic Substances and Hazardous and 
Nuclear Wastes Control Act of 1990, was 
enacted to respond to increasing problems 
associated 
with 
toxic 
chemicals 
and 
hazardous and nuclear wastes. It is the policy 
of the State to regulate, restrict or prohibit the 
importation, manufacture, processing, sale, 
distribution, use, and disposal of chemical 
substances and mixtures that present 
unreasonable risk and/or injury to health 
or the environment; to prohibit the entry, 
even in transit, of hazardous and nuclear 
wastes and their disposal into the Philippine 
territorial limits for whatever purpose; and to 
provide advancement and facilitate research 
and studies on toxic chemicals.
Its Implementing Rules and Regulations, 
Department of Environment and Natural 
Resources (DENR) Administrative Order 
(DAO) 1992-29 is divided into two (2) 
main sections: Title II – Toxic Chemical 
Substances and Title III – Hazardous and 
Nuclear Wastes. Title II comprises the 
chemical management system including the 
regulations of chemicals under the Priority 
Chemical List (PCL) and Chemical Control 
Orders (CCO) while Title III comprises 
the hazardous waste management which 
include the regulations for hazardous wastes 
generators, transporters, and TSD facilities.

unreasonable risk to public health workplace, 
and the environment.  On the other hand,

9.3 BUDGET AND EXPENDITURE 
2022 BUDGET ALLOCATION
The budget allocated for EMB Region 3 for 
CY 2022 is PhP 325.977 million. The biggest 
chunk of the budget was allotted for Capital 
Outlay (CO) in the amount of PhP 173.040 
million which included the allocation for 
the installation of trash traps worth PhP 
150.40 million and PhP 16.800 million for 
the establishment of preliminary treatment 
and storage facility for Covid 19 wastes. 
Operating expenses in the amount of PhP 
84.678 million was allocated in the 2022 
budget and include expenses covering 
office and laboratory supplies, traveling 
allowances, rental fees, utility expenses (i.e. 
power, water, fuel) repair of equipment and 
vehicles, and other operational costs in the 
implementation of EMB mandates.
Table 59 indicated the distribution of the 
amount allotted for programs and projects 
mandated to be implemented by EMB. As 
reflected on the table, the largest amount 
goes to the Implementation of Solid Waste 
Management Regulations. The budget for 
the Manila Bay Rehabilitation Program was 
already integrated in the regular fund (Fund 
101) allocation on the Implementation of 
Clean Water Regulations. 
9.4 REVENUES GENERATED
Revenues generated by the Regional 
Office comes from regulatory fees and 
permit applications as a requirement of 
environmental laws such as PD 1586, RA 
8749, RA 9275 and RA 6969 as well as 
from the payment of fines and penalties for 
violations committed by industrial firms.
Despite the persistence of the Covid-19 
pandemic and the difficult economic 
situation, EMB Region 3 still managed to 
collect revenues for the government. A 
total of four thousand two hundred and 
fifty-three (4,253) Notices of Violations 
were issued for the year with the highest 
collection for fines and penalties collected 
from the implementation of PD 1586. 
EMB Region 3 was able to generate more 
than PhP 41.592 million from the collection 
of fines and penalties. Regulatory fees 
amounting to more than PhP 23.699 million 
came from permit fee applications. Total 
revenues generated for the year amounted 
to more than PhP 65.292 million for the 
government despite the challenges and the 
economic effects brought by the continuing 
pandemic.

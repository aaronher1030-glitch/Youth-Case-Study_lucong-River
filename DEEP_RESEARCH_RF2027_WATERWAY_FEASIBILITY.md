## 2. Site screening and recommended scope [Confidence: Medium for historical evidence; Low for current site feasibility]

### Estero de Paco: first verification candidate

Clemente's registered abstract identifies Paco as a feeder into the Pasig River and associates elevated biochemical oxygen demand during heavy rains with septic-waste discharge. It describes bridges and residential areas as potential sampling locations, but the retrieved abstract does not name them. An ADB-hosted page describes the whole estero as three kilometers long. Those facts establish a plausible waterway-scale candidate, not a verified one-kilometer project boundary. [2,3]

Paco is preferable for the initial screen because the intervention can be framed within an existing estero-management context and scaled to other Pasig-connected esteros. However, that same rehabilitation history creates a duplication risk. The first investigation must establish what the city, barangays and other programs already collect, which households actually lack dependable service, and whether a new project would add capacity rather than rebadge an existing effort. Historical household sewage also means that a successful litter service must not be advertised as comprehensive water treatment. [2,3]

The **proposed service population of 400 households is a costing assumption**, not a census or capacity finding. The selected households and all project storage/collection installations would need to lie within the eventual approved pilot footprint associated with the same contiguous 1-km reach. Do not use a one-kilometer label while quietly servicing a dispersed citywide catchment. Existing licensed off-site receiving facilities are necessary destinations, not additional project installations.

The precise upstream and downstream endpoints remain unselected. The required decision artifact is a verified centerline map with named endpoints, measured chainage of 0+000 to 1+000, drain connections, source locations, bank-access constraints, land ownership and the service footprint. A GIS measurement must be checked against field conditions; straight-line bridge spacing is not river length. No coordinates have been invented.

### Other candidates and why they do not currently win

**Estero de San Miguel** is a reasonable fallback waterway, but the retrieved account documents 2018 dredging, parks and phytoremediation rather than a current source inventory or performance baseline. It does not establish a current responsible implementer or a specific accessible kilometer. Choosing it now would exchange one uncertain site for a less documented one. [5]

**Tullahan** has stronger quantitative litter evidence. Talavera and colleagues report floating-plastic flux estimates of 308–1,226 items/hour at selected locations. Floating observations occurred in May 2023, with bank collection in June; submerged material was excluded from visual counts. These are neither annual loads nor Paco observations. The retrieved monitored bridge stations are not a verified eligible tributary/creek pilot; use them to motivate a later Tullahan site screen, not to invent a local baseline or claim all Tullahan reaches have that rate. [4]

**Meycauayan** is a weaker first transfer target for a trash-only pilot. Its historical water and sediment assessment found low dissolved oxygen and metal contamination. That does not establish current exposure levels, but it is enough to reject the idea that visible litter removal alone constitutes industrial-pollution restoration. No sediment disturbance or student hazardous-waste handling is proposed. [6]

### Conditions that reject the site

Reject a candidate if there is no lawful and flood-safe access, no confirmed receiving facility, no identifiable service deficit, or no practical way to serve the chosen footprint. Also reject a claim of household-source dominance if reconnaissance instead identifies imported upstream litter or industrial/sewage loads as the main problem relevant to the selected outcome. Site selection is reversible; a persuasive story is not a substitute for these conditions.

If Paco fails, San Miguel must pass the same checks from the beginning. No permissions, costs, baseline or expected effect transfer automatically. If neither can be adequately verified before the competition's internal freeze, the honest outcome is an incomplete site-feasibility case—not a fabricated final map.

## 3. Intervention comparison and emerging approaches [Confidence: Medium]

### Recommended architecture: reliable service with a verified waste destination

The recommended bundle is ordinary but operationally explicit: source-separated household storage, a mapped accessible collection route, a named service provider, covered temporary transfer where lawful, scheduled transport, a confirmed receiving facility, delivery receipts, and a response to missed service. Separate ordinary residuals and recyclables from suspect hazardous material. Do not mix segregated streams again during hauling. Philippine waste law makes the downstream division of responsibilities an essential part of the intervention. [13]

This is a service-and-protocol proposal with modest equipment, not a speculative device project. The hypothesized mechanism is that dependable collection reduces the need or opportunity for local dumping, and that verified final delivery prevents merely shifting waste from the bank to another unregulated location. The local effect remains unmeasured. An institutional case on leakage mapping illustrates the value of diagnosing pathways before selecting infrastructure, but supplies no transferable effect size. [22]

No river-spanning barrier, drain closure, dredging, aeration system, artificial wetland, wastewater diversion, custom sensor network or app is necessary for the initial package. Keeping them out protects the budget and causal story. Basic weighing, service logs and repeatable observations have priority over a dashboard whose inputs are unreliable.

### Why interception is not the default

Honingh et al. demonstrate how plastic accumulation at racks can raise upstream water levels, using laboratory and Indonesian field evidence. The proper inference is a general obstruction hazard, not a specific flood prediction for Paco. In a densely occupied urban drainage setting, an unattended capture device can trade visible litter retention for an unquantified hydraulic liability. [8]

Accordingly, the base design has **no in-channel installation**. If later engineering work identifies a genuinely off-channel, non-obstructive source interceptor, it requires a separate approval and maintenance decision; it is not a hidden component of this budget. Collection-only remains a viable design. The project should not depend on workers clearing blocked structures during dangerous storms.

### Wetlands, aeration, treatment plants and industrial sensing

A recent floating-treatment-wetland review identifies retention time, coverage, plant establishment, depth and placement as important design considerations. Its reviewed abstract supports design dependence, not a guarantee of treating a flowing estero. Without hydraulic residence, safe anchoring, maintainable vegetation and contaminant characterization, a plant island is an illustration rather than an engineered treatment system. The full review was inaccessible, so no numerical removal efficiencies are imported. [9]

The Philippine sanitation sourcebook supports selecting treatment trains for actual wastewater flows and local maintenance capacity. Decentralized treatment can be a better choice than collection when a bounded sewage source, usable land, a responsible operator and suitable discharge arrangements exist. None has been established here. Diverting the entire river into a small treatment device is not a credible substitute for characterizing an outfall. [10,14]

Aeration may improve oxygen conditions locally but does not itself remove all incoming pollutants. Industrial sensors may flag an anomaly but do not establish every pollutant, legal liability or corrective action. A commercial/industrial option would need a regulator-backed sampling, confirmatory analysis and enforcement workflow before equipment procurement. These alternatives are not rejected universally; they are less justified by the currently available project-specific evidence. [10,14]

## 4. Legal, operating and financial feasibility [Confidence: Medium]

### Authority and waste-chain obligations

RA9003 section 10 identifies barangay responsibilities for biodegradable, compostable and reusable wastes, while municipality/city responsibility includes collecting non-recyclable and special wastes. Section 32 addresses MRFs and residual transfer; sections 37 and 48 constrain dumping and improper collection/disposal. The operational implication is a written division of work and a compliant destination, not an assumption that a barangay endorsement legalizes the whole chain. [13]

The actual city environmental office, engineering office, barangays, land/asset owners and receiving operator must be identified. DENR-EMB involvement, and DPWH/MMDA or other asset-manager involvement, depend on jurisdiction and works. The team cannot assign authority merely by listing agency logos. A sampling activity is not automatically a wastewater-discharge operation; a treatment/diversion scheme would require separate assessment under the applicable water-pollution rules. [14]

The Water Code's urban easement is not a license to build. Article 51 identifies a three-meter public-use zone and also restricts structures and occupation. Covered storage or equipment therefore needs its own lawful site, outside prohibited placement unless a competent authority determines otherwise. No relocation or displacement is part of the recommendation. [15]

### Worker and community protection

RA11058 requires appropriate free PPE for covered employment but expressly excludes the public sector from its coverage. Private-contractor workers, ordinary LGU employees, students and people called volunteers should not be collapsed into one legal category. The implementation owner must establish the correct employment and safety framework. Regardless of status, this plan does not permit unprotected handling, confined-space entry or dangerous flood operations. [16]

CDC guidance supports hygiene, training, appropriate protective equipment and locally advised vaccination for workers handling sewage-associated material. The proposed work uses adult trained crews, bank-based observations, clean washing facilities, incident procedures and stop-work authority. Students do not enter water or drains, sort sharps, disturb sediment, operate risky equipment or inspect suspected industrial discharges. [18]

Household records should be minimal and coded. Avoid identifiable images, unnecessary names, exact household-level public maps and unconsented publicity. A privacy notice and separate publication permission are recommended safeguards; the retrieved NPC guidance does not establish that consent is the sole lawful basis for every processing activity or that a research exemption automatically applies. [21]

Principal assumptions are 400 enrolled households, six adult collection workers for ten operating months, eight handcarts, four lawful covered transfer points, a 12-month supervisor and contracted hauling. No household count, local waste mass, route productivity, storage availability or supplier quote has been observed. These numbers define a testable planning scenario, not a design ready for purchase.

The wage calculation assumes ₱900 per worker-day, 26 paid days per month for ten months, and a provisional 25% employer-cost allowance. ## 5. Outcomes, baseline and attribution [Confidence: Medium for methodological cautions; Low for local effect]

### Separate service delivery from environmental effects

Count households actually served, completed pickups, response to missed service, mass transferred, receiving receipts, overflow and downtime. These can show that the service operated. They cannot by themselves show how much river leakage was prevented: some collected waste may previously have been properly disposed of, and the same load can be weighed at several transfers. Count it once in a defined flow ledger and retain destinations.

The proposed environmental estimand is the change in **downstream-direction visible floating-litter passage rate at a fixed boundary station**, compared with baseline and untreated observation locations under comparable conditions. It is not total river plastic mass, net annual export or all pollution removal. Flow reversals are recorded separately; a tidally recirculating item may pass more than once. The detectable size class, observation width, timing and classification rules must be fixed after a measurement trial. Tullahan's study explicitly excludes submerged litter, illustrating this observational limitation. [4]

The provisional engineering objective is a 20% reduction in standardized passage rate, alongside at least 95% scheduled service completion, assessed over the fixed evaluation window and counting rules defined in the implementation plan. These are proposed targets, not literature-derived local predictions. A simple non-tidal illustration shows the constraint: if local controllable sources contribute 40% of the downstream baseline and the service halves those sources, the downstream change is 20%. Both percentages are hypothetical; tidal storage, storms and source substitution can invalidate the simple relationship.

### Baseline design and sample schedule

The proposed twelve-month pilot reserves months 1–2 for permissions, characterization and an eight-week pre-intervention baseline. Twice-weekly fixed observations continue through the year. The costing scenario allows three impact-reach stations and two comparison observation stations, with monthly water-quality rounds at five locations, 60 planned sample events and six additional QA sample events. Sampling packages and laboratory recognition are unverified until quotations and methods are confirmed.

Comparison observations outside the pilot must not introduce project installations or extend the intervention footprint. Confirm that this interpretation satisfies the kit. If safe comparable locations or approved secondary records are unavailable, report a weaker interrupted time series; do not imply a randomized or replicated treatment experiment. An upstream station characterizes incoming loads and is not automatically a valid untreated control.

MRC's protocol supports standardized records, seasonal coverage and the limits of short monitoring periods. It does not prescribe the exact cadence above, which is a planning choice. An eight-week baseline cannot establish an entire wet/dry seasonal counterfactual. Avoid attributing changes caused by rainfall, tides, unrelated cleanups or upstream construction to the project. [11]

For water quality, measure field DO, temperature, conductivity/salinity and turbidity where appropriate, and obtain method-specified laboratory BOD, TSS and a suitable fecal indicator. Without measured flow, report concentrations rather than pollutant loads. These are diagnostic outcomes in a trash-service pilot; no bathing-safety, disease-reduction, metal-removal or wastewater-compliance promise follows from an improvement in visible litter. [2,6,10]

### Analysis and honest failure criteria

Before intervention, freeze the primary outcome, target, observation schedule, exclusions and comparison strategy. Examine baseline variability, temporal correlation, detection limits and comparison-site behavior before choosing the final model. A repeated-count model may be appropriate, but no power, minimum detectable effect or empirical confidence interval can be calculated credibly without observations. Synthetic sensitivity analysis, if used later, must be unmistakably labeled synthetic.

Smokorowski and Randall's accessible abstract shows that BACI conclusions can depend on the configuration of monitoring years. Its full text was inaccessible, so more specific claims are not presented as verified quotations from that paper. As design safeguards, distinguish repeated observations from independent treated sites, account for temporal dependence, and keep inference local to this one pilot. [12]

Predefine three possible findings and the exact classification inequalities before intervention: the practical target is supported if the effect interval's lower bound reaches the target; there is evidence against achieving the target if the interval's upper bound falls below it; otherwise the result is inconclusive for the target. The implementation plan fixes the effect calculation, comparison strategy, evaluation window and service-counting rules. A result below the target may still show some benefit; it is not proof of no effect. Do not switch outcomes, alter exclusions or retune the target after seeing unfavorable results.

## 7. Open questions and red-team assessment [Confidence: Low for unresolved items]

The strongest objection is that this may be an expensive collection program duplicating work already funded. The response must be an observed service gap and incremental cost-effectiveness analysis, not a new name for routine cleanup. A second objection is that improved service may barely change downstream litter because most material originates upstream; this can invalidate the environmental target even if the crews perform well. A third is that sewage, rather than trash, drives the most important local health problem. Paco's historical paper makes that objection particularly credible. [2,3]

The financial model has no quotations, actual waste volumes, confirmed storage parcels or receiving contract. Its sizable contingency improves tolerance of cost uncertainty but cannot create land rights, operator capacity or a viable waste destination. A nominally affordable service is still infeasible if no one can lawfully and reliably deliver it.

A fourth objection concerns competition fit: reliable service is not nationally novel, and the AI declaration may preclude using this substantive assistance altogether. The answer is not concealment. Obtain an authoritative ruling, preserve the true provenance, and accept that this dossier may be usable only for learning. [1,3]

## Methodology

This was a standard-depth, decision-oriented research run, not a commissioned engineering feasibility study. The user delegated location and approach selection. Five areas were examined: kit compliance; sites and national context; intervention effectiveness and failure modes; legal and financial feasibility; and measurement and attribution. Three independent retrieval agents handled site/statistical evidence, technology/evaluation, and legal/cost evidence. One targeted gap-fill agent examined Paco, and one independent verification agent audited ten high-impact claim groups.

Search seeds included: Estero de Paco water quality station map; Estero de San Miguel rehabilitation; Tullahan macroplastic survey; Meycauayan industrial metals; Meijer Philippines model uncertainty; plastic interception flood risk; household collection leakage; floating wetland hydraulic limitations; river macroplastic monitoring protocol; BACI environmental monitoring cautions; RA9003 waste responsibilities; Water Code easements; NCR wage orders 2026; Philippine laboratory fees; and lawful hauling costs. Critical evidence was sought deliberately rather than treating every intervention as beneficial.

The final bibliography contains 22 sources, including the provided kit. Tier 1 identifies primary laws, official technical/statistical material and appropriate primary scholarly records; Tier 2 identifies credible institutional or journalistic accounts. Tier describes provenance, not whether a particular page was fully read. Historical/site evidence is dated; old methodological work is retained as foundational; recent review evidence is explicitly limited to accessible abstracts where necessary. No current-affordability claim rests on a stale laboratory fee or unrelated hauling tender.

The quality review found that source coverage was adequate for conditional option selection but not for current site approval. Another generic retrieval wave would not replace a field survey, operating agreement or quote. Thus, the report retained unresolved gates rather than inflating source count. The original structure was adapted to emphasize competition compliance, site screening and feasibility instead of a broad trends survey.

Verification corrections included: Clemente is a single author; model emissions refer to 2015 with mixed-vintage inputs; the two wastewater percentages cannot be reconciled by relabeling denominators; the NCR litigation qualification concerns a separate displayed order; RA9003 section 10 says reusable, not recyclable; RA11058 excludes the public sector; and the urban easement is not installation permission. Full kit text was extracted using PyMuPDF because the PDF reader lacked its rendering dependency. Six scholarly citations were checked directly against Crossref metadata. Selected source passages were independently inspected by the verification agent, while other retrievals remained summarized, abstract-only or snippet-only as recorded below.

Confidence labels do not convert the kit into multiple independent sources. A single authoritative source is sufficient to identify what its own rules say, but it cannot establish a local engineering outcome. No effect, cost, site-access claim or implementation success has been field-verified. The report's recommendations, scenario quantities, schedule, decision weights and proposed targets are analysis, not published facts.
